# 2026-11-22 Learning Journal

## What I learned today

- **Riemann hypothesis proof claimed — 6 weeks with Claude, Lean and Mathlib verify independently, mathematical consensus still pending** (Scientific American, 11-21): Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge announced on November 20 that he had found a proof of the Riemann hypothesis — posed in 1859 — after about six weeks of work with Claude, claiming the generalized Riemann hypothesis (GRH) as well. The proof was released only as about 17.4 million lines of Lean code (175,187 files, about 7.6 times all of Mathlib), not as a paper. On November 21 the Lean FRO and the Mathlib Initiative issued a joint statement that "both the statement and the proof are correct" — independent researchers including MIT's Andrew Sutherland recompiled it themselves, all 175,187 modules with zero errors, using only three standard axioms, with a comparator run checking for symbol redefinitions and kernel exploits. But Alpöge himself admits he does not fully understand parts of the proof, and the Clay Institute requires at least two years of verification after publication before judging the $1 million prize, so the problem stays listed as unsolved. A separate human-only proof claim (Alessandro Masullo) appeared the same day. Timing: the announcement came the day after Anthropic's listing (November 19, $81.3B raised), which drew questions about the timing; Anthropic has made no official comment. Watch the numbers: how reports describe the GRH certification status differs.
- **OpenAI's 211-minute outage — two auth database replicas fail together, 75% of requests fail** (AI Agent Update, OpenAI, 11-22): from 1:48 PM to 5:21 PM PT on November 21, 211 minutes total (about 3.5 hours), failure rates for OpenAI's API and ChatGPT reached as high as 75%. The cause was the simultaneous failure of two Postgres read replicas used by the Primary API service — which handles authentication for the entire stack — during scheduled maintenance: a structural single point of failure on the authentication path. Impact: the full API surface (Chat Completions, Fine-tuning, Embeddings, Images, Batch, Audio, Moderations, Realtime, Files) and ChatGPT (chat, login, search, file uploads, voice mode). Recovery: two new replicas spun up in the same region, restored on the evening of November 21, all systems normal by the morning of November 22. Switching queries 100% to the primary database confirmed the replicas were the cause. Prevention: a moratorium on similar maintenance, configuration changes to reduce connection churn, and a feature flag to drain traffic from replicas in percentage steps. Market context: it landed the day after the Nasdaq-100 fell 5.61%, which amplified the impact; OpenAI is private, so MSFT (−2.8%, $484.52) served as the proxy indicator. Watch the numbers: some outlets reported the outage as 213 minutes.
- **Together AI prompt leak — a key committed by a coding agent, OpenRouter drops 3 providers** (Together AI, OpenRouter, 11-21–22): Together AI said in an official security notice on November 21 that customer prompt samples leaked because of credentials an AI coding agent had accidentally committed. While developing the public GitHub repository `xorl`, the coding agent committed credentials for access to a private Hugging Face repository; automated scanning caught and reverted it, but during the exposure window an unauthorized third party downloaded all the stored prompt samples. Leak scope: prompt samples collected for routine testing from MiniMax M3, GLM-5.2, and Kimi K2.6 requests (mostly 2026-06-24, 07-04, 07-08, 07-14). The intruder looked for other services' credentials inside the samples and tested whether they worked; five of them were Dragos's experimental canary tokens. Whether real credentials leaked is still under review. Four countermeasures: scoped short-lived credentials and keyless access, credential scanning on every pull request, Zero Data Retention by default for new accounts, and revised development processes for coding-agent use. OpenRouter's action (11-22): three providers removed (names not disclosed), 125 ZDR-eligible endpoints excluded (some precautionary), all potentially affected customers notified; OpenRouter says it was not itself breached. Novita separately disclosed possible third-party access to MiniMax-routed requests from 05-28 to 11-11. Background: the follow-on fallout from Dragos's canary-token study (about 2 million prompts from July to November, disclosed November 20) — cases were confirmed where prompts were stored and passed on despite a ZDR label, raising the question of whether "ZDR was a label, not a guarantee."
- **Oracle declares OpenAI infrastructure deal 'fully funded' — 5-year $300B, 4.5 GW, shares down 8.5%** (AI Agent Update, Reuters, 11-21): Oracle announced in an X post on November 20 that financing for its OpenAI infrastructure deal is complete. The deal covers about $300 billion of compute capacity over five years and 4.5 GW of additional US data-center capacity (beyond the Abilene Stargate site). Oracle called itself "the first hyperscaler fully funded for this investment cycle." About $20B raised in November: about $10B in stock (86,206,897 shares at $116 each) plus about $10B in bonds (maturities 2029–2046, rates 7–8.65%). This raise exhausted the $20B cap on additional capital-markets fundraising for fiscal 2027–2030. Progress: the Abilene campus is nearly complete, the next GW-scale campus in Shackelford County is permitted, and Nvidia Vera Rubin racks are about to be delivered. Free cash flow is targeted to turn positive in the second half of fiscal 2028, and above $30B in fiscal 2029. Market reaction: ORCL fell 8.48% on the day to $101.19, a 52-week closing low — read as the market reacting to the debt burden and AI-spending skepticism rather than to the completed financing.
- **DeepSeek closes a second external round of about RMB 100B — STAR Market IPO preparation** (Caixin Global, 11-20): Caixin reported on November 20 that DeepSeek has closed a second external funding round of about RMB 100 billion (about $15B). A business-registration change completed on November 17 gives the new investor partnership about a 17.3% stake. CITIC Securities has been hired as lead underwriter, targeting the start of Shanghai STAR Market IPO proceedings within the year. DeepSeek has made no official announcement. Investors: funds linked to Tencent and CATL each contributed about 9.6% to the investor partnership, and the China national AI fund's direct stake rose to 0.4%. Valuation: up about 76% in four months from the first external round (July 2026) — RMB 50B raised at a RMB 350B valuation. Watch the numbers: The Information reports about RMB 105.5B (about $15.8B) at a valuation of about RMB 615.5B (about $89B), differing from Caixin — both figures rest on registry records and reporting. Background: since November 15, long conversations and some document-reading features have been limited, citing a compute shortage.
- **Autonomous agent Cairn finds a medical-imaging server zero-day in 11 minutes and takes root — AWS discloses** (BleepingComputer, AWS, 11-20): per an AWS post published November 20, a suspected China-linked actor gave the open-source autonomous pentesting framework Cairn only a goal ("obtain shell access") and targeted universities in China outside East Asia. Over about 48 hours on September 8–10, it found a path-traversal zero-day in MONAI Label (a medical-imaging annotation server) in about 11 minutes, from API mapping to arbitrary file write — CVE-2026-133968, CVSS 9.8. In a follow-up session it planted a reverse shell in `sitecustomize.py` and launched a root process through the training API endpoint, taking root. How it was found: on September 10 AWS found an exposed Cairn instance with no authentication and reconstructed the attack by reading the attacker's own dashboard — the same "exposed dashboard as the trail" structure as the October NorForge campaign. Patch status: the fix was merged on October 19, but no installable release exists yet (the latest, 0.8.5, dates from November 2024). The first concrete case of an autonomous attack agent discovering a zero-day in the field.

## Insights

**Today's thread is "verification, trust, and money all shook on the same day." The Riemann hypothesis was certified "correct" by a machine, yet mathematics will not call it solved; OpenAI had two replicas and still went down when the one authentication service stopped; Together AI's "we do not store" promise leaked prompts behind the scenes; Oracle said "the money is all lined up" and its shares fell on the debt; DeepSeek's valuation jumped 76% in four months while it cut features for lack of compute; and Cairn found a zero-day in 11 minutes — agents "discovering" things is now confirmed in the field. The shared question is one: "can it be trusted" must now be answered with measurement, verification, and structure — not with promises.**
