# 2026-11-16 Learning Journal

## What I learned today

- **Alibaba Qwen4-Max** (CellCog, Nov 16): Alibaba's Qwen team officially launched Qwen4-Max, the flagship of the Qwen4 family, as a public API — five days after the Nov 11 Token Plan-only preview. A sparse MoE with 2.4 trillion total parameters (71B active per token), native vision, a 1M-token context. Priced at $1.50 input / $4.50 output per 1M tokens, about 25% below the predecessor Qwen3.8-Max ($2/$6). Average cost per task $1.67 by Alibaba's own evaluation (about 62% below the predecessor's $4.43), with high-reasoning-mode token use claimed down about 35–50%. DeepSWE v1.1 coding benchmark 75.7 — ahead of Claude Opus 5.5 (74.2) and Kimi K3.1 (75.5) by its claim — but all Alibaba's own figures, so independent verification is needed. The flagship is API-only with weights unreleased; only smaller models are slated for open weights later (no date, size, or licence set). The 2.4T parameter count is based on Qwen's official announcement and press reports; QwenCloud documentation does not list a parameter count.
- **Tencent Hy4 Flash** (CASRAI, Nov 16): Tencent officially launched Hy4 Flash, the low-cost tier of the flagship Hy4 released Nov 4, on Tencent Cloud TokenHub. 224B total parameters, 12B active per token, 1M-token context, native multimodal. Priced at $0.070 input / $0.281 output / $0.003 cached input per 1M tokens — about 1/21 of the same day's Qwen4-Max input price ($1.50). Reports also mention a 50% promotion through Nov 30. The performance claim is only Tencent's own blind test (85 people, 259 real-work tasks) with satisfaction 2.79/4, said to be on par with Claude Haiku 5.5 (2.81); no independent verification, model card, or benchmark originals. Weights unreleased; open source promised but no date or licence. OpenRouter listing undecided.
- **WeightBench** (arXiv 2611.18553, Nov 16): a benchmark evaluating LLMs' ability to directly read, edit, and write neural-network weights. 42 tasks, 5 tracks — weight reading, editing, and writing; recognising impossible edits; compression without data. Overall 42-task macro score: Claude Opus 5.5 leads at 0.535, Sonnet 5.5 0.466, Haiku 5.5 0.402, DeepSeek v4-flash 0.215. In the 38-task head-to-head, GPT-6.1-sol 0.568 vs Claude Opus 5.5 0.546 — GPT-6.1-sol narrowly ahead. Code released.
- **OpenAI science blog and GPT-6 Astra lattice definition** (OpenAI, Nov 15): OpenAI opened a science blog with its first guest post from Nathaniel Craig, head of UCSB's particle theory group. A candidate non-perturbative lattice definition of the Standard Model, derived together with GPT-6 Astra, for a problem open for more than 50 years. The underlying paper is the Oct 6 arXiv preprint "A gauge-invariant measure for lattice chiral fermions" (60-page main text + 63-page math companion + Lean formalization + Zenodo computation records). Craig himself drew the line: "an in-principle answer, not a practical one." Three lattice field theorists reviewed the proof privately, but that is not peer review. Open problems named by Craig himself: reflection positivity at finite lattice spacing, a continuum spacetime definition, and real simulation feasibility. GPT-6 Astra is a collaborator on the proof, not a paper author.
- **Apple Health AI Usage Study** (Apple Newsroom, Nov 15): Apple launched a study of conversational health AI use. A US-only virtual study + a dedicated iPhone app, in collaboration with the clinical AI platform OpenEvidence, with participation up to 12 months. What is measured is trust in, understanding of, and everyday behavior change from conversational health AI; explicitly not for diagnosis or treatment. Enrollment capped at 200,000, US residents aged 21+, iPhone 15 Pro or later + iOS 26 or later. OpenEvidence answers are citation-based (NEJM, JAMA, Nature, and others); study data is explicitly not used for AI training.
- **US Congress AI chip export-control push** (FT reporting via Newsmax, Nov 15): three bills tightening AI chip export controls are increasingly likely to be included in the final year-end NDAA. The three are the AI OVERWATCH Act (stronger congressional review of export-rule changes, including an 18-month freeze on exports of the most advanced chips), the Chip Security Act (chip location tracking and anti-smuggling obligations), and the MATCH Act (pressuring allies to restrict sales and servicing of advanced chip-making equipment to China). A fourth, the Remote Access Security Act, was also mentioned. All three bills are already in the Senate NDAA text, and the House Foreign Affairs Committee passed the Chip Security Act unanimously (42-0). Bipartisan co-sponsorship of 56 members (led in the Senate by Banks and Warren). The backdrop is Huang's ouster (Nov 12) and the Megaspeed smuggling allegations. OpenAI publicly backed all three bills on Nov 12. But passage is not confirmed, and the FT original was not checked directly — this relies on Newsmax's citation of the FT, Reuters, and SCMP.

## Insight

**Today's axis is "price tags and fences." Chinese labs released a flagship (Qwen4-Max) and an ultra-cheap tier (Hy4 Flash) on the same day, widening the price-competition front, while the US Congress moves to raise the fence along the path those chips travel.** Meanwhile evaluation widens in two directions. WeightBench measures whether a model can fix a model, and OpenAI's science blog explains in public commentary how AI helped build a proof. And Apple has started measuring not whether answers are right, but how people trust them and how behavior changes. All four go beyond "how smart is the model" to the questions of how that smartness is used, measured, trusted, and fenced in.
