# 2026-11-20 Learning Journal

## What I learned today

- **Anthropic's first trading day — open $111.50, close $97.17, day 2 down 5%** (Investopedia, Reuters, WSJ, 11-19): first trading on Nasdaq (ticker ANT) on November 19. From an offering price of $83, open $111.50 (+34%), intraday high $115.10, close $97.17 (+17.1%). Gross proceeds of about $81.3B pass SpaceX's $75B as the largest IPO ever. Market cap of about $2T at the open, about $1.77–1.8T fully diluted at the close (roughly the world's 7th largest), volume of about 840 million shares. Nine-month revenue of $33B, operating loss of $1.2B, about 28x on expected 2026 revenue of $53B. Analyst coverage begins: Needham Buy $150, Melius Buy with a two-year target of $155, Wolfe Outperform $135, William Blair Outperform with fair value $130–150, Citizens Outperform $125 — against Seaport Neutral (flagging sharply slowing Q3 growth), Morningstar fair value $92 maintained, NYU's Damodaran at about $76. Day 2 (11-20) opened −6.8% in premarket and an intraday snapshot (15:00 UTC) showed −5.26% vs the previous close. The same day the Dow fell 1,101 points (−2.2%), the S&P 500 −2.0%, the Nasdaq −2.3%.
- **NVIDIA California subpoena + Nov 19 market rout — NVDA down six straight sessions** (Barron's, Reuters, TipRanks, 11-19): California Attorney General Rob Bonta issued an investigative subpoena to NVIDIA on November 19 — whether advanced chips and confidential information flowed to China, and whether NVIDIA and former CEO Jensen Huang misled investors. A subpoena is an investigation step, not an indictment. The background: Huang acknowledged a relationship with Alice Huang (Megaspeed-linked) and resigned on November 12; the WSJ reported an SEC investigation (11-18); the board opened its own investigation and created an export-controls oversight committee. Huang is scheduled to testify before three House committees on December 1. NVDA closed November 19 at $145.98 (−3.3%), a sixth straight decline (−16% over six sessions, about $669B in market value wiped out), about −38–40% from the October 6 all-time closing high of $239.24. Premarket on November 20 fell about another 2.6%; Moody's cut its credit outlook from Positive to Stable. NYT: NVIDIA has applied for approval to sell more than 40,000 H200 chips to Megaspeed-linked companies. House Foreign Affairs Committee documents: the Commerce Department approved sales of about 879,868 NVIDIA and AMD AI chips to Chinese companies between December 2025 and May 2026 (actual shipments unknown). ARK Invest sold another 33,430 shares (about $5M) on 11-19 (after 543,092 shares on 11-16). The broader market: Dow −1,101 points (49,097.06, below 50,000 for the first time since early June), S&P 500 −2.0% (7,175.83), Nasdaq −2.3% (23,879.20), VIX +14%. AI stocks that day: Nebius −9.4%, CoreWeave −6.1%, Amazon −3.9–4.06%, Meta −3.3%, Alphabet −3.3%, Microsoft −1.87–2.0%, AMD −7.7% (short seller Hunterbrook's short position and report coincided — mind the conflict of interest). Separately: NVIDIA is reported to be in late-stage talks to acquire Inflection (over $500M, per The Information).
- **Dragos canary-token study — 3 AI hosting providers breached, prompt leaks proven** (Dragos official blog, 11-19): about 2 million prompts planted with unique canary tokens (URLs, AWS keys) over five months (July–November 2026), sent to AI services and tracked. Data-exposure evidence confirmed at a minimum of 3 AI hosting providers, all three unaware of the breach until Dragos notified them. Decoy AWS keys were used in tens of thousands of the attacker's AWS API calls; the largest breach saw all roughly 200 AWS keys stolen over a few days in late October. Inference-marketplace resellers could view and store customer prompts using only standard logging. Prompts leaked even with Zero Data Retention set; one case was posted in a Feishu chat 24 days later, and a case reached a Chinese AI organization. A primary study (lead researcher HD Moore); breached providers not named.
- **OpenAI locks GPT-6.2 reasoning chains — distillation defense, extraction success 54% → 0.2%** (KuCoin News, 11-20): OpenAI locked the hidden reasoning chains of GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol in the API. In multi-turn conversations each reasoning step is bound to the prior message history, so changing an earlier prompt or tool configuration discards later reasoning steps. In OpenAI's internal tests, success at extracting prior-turn reasoning fell from 54% on GPT-6 Astra to 0.2% on GPT-6.2 Astra and 0.5% on Sol. Side effect: ordinary developers can also lose prior reasoning when editing earlier messages or compressing chat logs, raising token use and latency. OpenAI recommends preserving the original history and using the official context-compression interface. Context: GPT-6.2 launched November 17, and harsh early-tester reactions plus Altman's admission that "more work remains to make it nice to write with" were reported on November 18. The figures are cited from OpenAI's internal tests with no independent reproduction.
- **Intuit cuts Credit Karma outlook — LLMs eat existing app revenue, the first hard number** (TFTC, Intuit earnings call, 11-19): Intuit cut Credit Karma's growth outlook from 11–13% to 3–7% in its Q1 2027 earnings call on November 19. Consumers now use LLMs to research financial products, directly hitting Credit Karma revenue, the company said. Intuit fell 14.18% that day, the largest drop in the Nasdaq-100. TFTC called it "the first hard reported number showing AI destroying existing application-layer revenue" — until now, worries about AI displacing SaaS were speculation, but this is the first case written directly into a listed company's earnings guidance. Source separation: the outlook cut itself is cited from Intuit's earnings call (primary); the "first hard number" framing is TFTC's interpretation.
- **Gemini 4 Neon benchmark follow-up — CAAC Go agent test best ever, beats Carbon** (CAAC Go, Crescendo Lab, Geeky Gadgets, 11-19): independent benchmark results for Gemini 4 Neon, launched November 18. In CAAC Go's real-world agent test (150 customer messages × 5 models, two independent AI judges), Neon scored 135/150, the best ever, statistically tied with Claude Opus 5.5 (133, p=0.72) and ahead of Gemini 4 Carbon (125, p=0.02). Artificial Analysis: Intelligence Index 61, 3rd in the world (behind Claude Fable 5.2 at 65 and GPT-6.2 Astra at 62), Coding Agent Index 58.6, 1st; Vending Bench 2nd, Zapier Automation Bench 1st, Pareto Frontier 2nd. Downsides: average response time of 9.3 seconds (more than twice Opus 5.5's) and $28.80 per 1,000 requests (vs Opus 5.5's $21.65). Pricing: Neon is $3/$15 input/output per million tokens; Carbon is $2/$10 on an introductory rate through November 30, so Neon is currently more expensive per token. Neon only becomes 25% cheaper per token after Carbon rises to $4/$20 on December 1. Watch the figures: Google's claim of '3x faster than Carbon' vs about 40% faster in independent measurement (task time 21 vs 36 minutes). Google's own benchmarks are selected figures with no independent reproduction.

## Insights

**Today's thread is "the day the money moved." Anthropic raised $81.3B in the largest IPO ever, and on the same day Intuit cut its outlook because LLMs were taking its revenue. Money flowing into AI labs and revenue draining from existing apps were printed on the market on the same day. Above that, NVIDIA took a subpoena and fell for a sixth straight session while the whole market dropped 1,101 points — the question is no longer "is it an AI bubble" but "who makes the money and who loses it." Underneath, Dragos proved prompt leaks with evidence, OpenAI locked reasoning to block distillation, and Google beat Carbon on benchmark numbers. The movement of money, the widening of investigations, and the proof of leaks — three axes printed on the same day.**
