# 2026-11-27 Learning Journal

## What I learned today

- **Samsung prepares a private cloud 'SPAC' for Galaxy AI — found in One UI 9.5 code (Nov 26)** (Android Authority, Nov 26): Code analysis of One UI 9.5 Beta 1 (Galaxy S26 Ultra) found traces of Samsung's private-cloud AI framework 'SPAC'. It follows the same design as Apple Private Cloud Compute: requests are processed on a server but not linked to who sent them. Samsung has not officially announced it, and the feature is switched off. It was found as a SPAC SDK inside four apps — Photo Editor, IntelliVoiceService (voice and language services), Smart Capture, and PhotoRemasterService. The server-side gateway is named 'Arcane', and separate token, identity, and fraud-detection services are mentioned. The privacy design (code-based): (1) requests pass through a third-party relay, so Samsung can see what was asked but not who asked it. (2) The phone verifies the server software has not been tampered with (attestation). (3) Server behavior is recorded in a public log that cannot be quietly changed. (4) Devices authenticate with anonymous one-time passes, without revealing the user. An 'unattested' mode that skips some checks also exists but appears to be for internal testing. Models mentioned: Google Gemma 4, Alibaba Qwen 3.6, Black Forest Labs FLUX.2 klein 4B, and Samsung's own Gauss — code references only, with actual use unconfirmed. Connected features: much of Galaxy AI's cloud functionality, including AI Select, cloud photo erase and upscale, real-time translation, Writing Tools, Now Brief, Daily Briefing, and Bixby Work. Backend preparation is underway: SPAC server addresses have been updated in the public certificate log and a US relay has been added. November 27 update: Android Authority succeeded in getting SPAC to run in a limited way alongside AI Select on the Galaxy S26 Ultra.
- **OpenAI begins alpha testing 'Private Inference' — prompts hidden even during processing (Nov 25)** (Unite.AI, Nov 25): Sam Altman posted on X on November 25 — OpenAI has begun testing 'Private Inference' with a small number of business customers. The goal is to make prompts invisible to OpenAI at the very moment the model processes them. It is the second pillar of the Private Intelligence previewed at DevDay 2026 (September 29). Confidential computing + verifiable controls — prompts and responses run encrypted inside isolated hardware while the model processes them. Where most privacy promises concern how data is handled 'after the answer', this one targets 'during the answer'. Scope: a small alpha for business API customers. Not consumer ChatGPT. Pricing, supported models, and GA date all undisclosed. Interested companies contact @shekar4x (the OpenAI contact). Announced alongside: ZDR with Private Safety Processing — content flagged by safety review is stored encrypted in the customer's own cloud (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, OCI), and the customer must keep it for at least 30 days. Automated safety review is performed without OpenAI staff seeing the original content. White paper: the October 26 Private Safety Processing paper — keys are released only after runtime identity, hardware attestation, and software measurements are verified; internal keys rotate daily and are destroyed after 30 days; measurements are recorded in a tamper-resistant log. But the standard safety runtime does not guarantee 'Zero Operator Access across the full inference', the paper states. Expansion plans: Altman said OpenAI is securing confidential-computing capacity for a large launch, with no timeline.
- **DeepL launches Aure v2, a direct speech-to-speech translation model — latency cut 50% (Nov 26)** (PR Newswire, Nov 26): DeepL launched its voice AI translation model Aure v2 at the first Unmute Summit in Tokyo on November 26. It is an end-to-end design that turns speech directly into translated speech without the three steps of speech → text → translation → speech, with median translation latency reportedly 50% lower than its predecessor. Available immediately to DeepL Voice customers. Structure: an end-to-end speech-to-speech model plus reinforcement learning. Removing the handoffs between transcription, translation, and speech synthesis cuts latency. The translated speech is said to preserve the speaker's rhythm, intonation, and emphasis. Figures (vendor claims): median translation latency down 50% vs the previous Aure. Based on DeepL's internal study (November 2026, 10 language pairs, 1-minute recordings of real speech, human-verified transcripts), half of the translated words are heard within 3.3 seconds. Quality claimed ahead of 6 competing systems across all language pairs — all vendor measurements, no independent verification. Languages: 6 at launch — English, German, French, Spanish, Japanese, Korean. Availability: DeepL Voice for Online Meetings (Teams, Zoom, Meet), Voice for In-Person Conversations, DeepL API for Voice. Enabled automatically in supported languages, with no separate setup. Pricing undisclosed. Pace: the previous Aure launched November 11 — the follow-up came just 15 days later. DeepL Voice usage in Q3 2026 was up more than 8x in voice minutes and about 7x in translated meeting minutes year over year.
- **Claude Duet expands to India, Indonesia, Brazil, and Japan — standalone iOS and Android apps (Nov 26)** (Gadgets360, Nov 26): Anthropic updated the Claude Duet product page, widening the research preview's country list to four countries beyond the US. Pro, Max, and Ultra subscribers in the US, India, Indonesia, Brazil, and Japan can now join the waitlist, and standalone iOS and Android apps are available in the stores. The first regional expansion since Duet's announcement (October 21). Scope: the November 25–26 product-page update added India, Indonesia, Brazil, and Japan. Pro/Max/Ultra subscribers, waitlist model retained. Waitlisted users are notified by email when admitted. Apps: standalone iOS (App Store) and Android (Google Play) apps. Three tabs: Chat, Tasks (live view of work in progress), and Make (a place to keep outputs). Work continues in the cloud with the phone locked. Features: Opus 5.5 based, 6,000+ connectors. Morning brief (important updates, missed notifications, messages), connector monitoring and alerts during the day, and draft replies via Gmail, Slack, and other integrations. User approval is required before sending email, making payments, or deleting. Plan status: personal Pro/Max/Ultra in research preview, Team in public beta, Enterprise in private beta (The Hindu BusinessLine, November 25). Caveat: Anthropic notes that, as a research preview, Duet can misread threads or misjudge urgency. The standalone apps themselves have been in the stores since early November; the news is the regional expansion.
- **Cloudflare launches 'Search for Agents' beta — websites as a search API for AI agents (Nov 25)** (Cloudflare Blog, Nov 25): Cloudflare launched Search for Agents in beta on November 25. A dashboard toggle makes your own website searchable by AI agents. Instead of crawling the site page by page, agents use the domain's own search endpoint. Available on all plans, including Free. How it works: enabling it from Quick Actions on the zone's Overview page auto-creates an AI Search instance (search-for-agents-<ZONE_ID>), crawls and indexes the site, and exposes a `https://<domain>/.well-known/search` endpoint. GET and HTTP QUERY (RFC 10008) requests are supported; results are Markdown content, not AI-generated answers. Discoverability: search-endpoint metadata (agent:endpoint, agent:method, and more) is auto-injected into the <head> of every HTML page, and into Markdown responses when Markdown for Agents is on. Agents can find the endpoint themselves and send keyword or natural-language queries. Search method: hybrid (vector + keyword) search, Discover crawling (sitemap + links, up to 100,000 pages, re-synced every 24 hours), embedding model @cf/qwen/qwen3-embedding-0.6b. Subdomains are excluded by default. Pricing: AI Search pricing applies; billing starts December 1, 2026. After the included allowance (1,000 semantic searches per month), hybrid queries cost $0.75 per 1,000 — the site owner pays every time an agent searches. Control: bot management, AI Crawl Control, and WAF settings continue to apply. Search finds information; it does not guarantee agents interpret or cite content correctly.
- **Gemini Live 'Guided Vision' expands to iPhone — spoken guidance from the camera (Nov 25)** (Digital Trends, Nov 25): Google's official Gemini account (@GeminiApp) posted on X on November 25 — Gemini Live's Guided Vision is now available on iOS too. It describes what the camera sees in spoken words and, when the framing is off, guides the camera position with words like 'turn slowly to the right'. The first platform expansion since the October 1 Android launch. How to use: open Gemini Live and share the camera; Gemini describes what it sees in real time and answers follow-up questions. If the camera is too high, too close, or off-target, voice guidance (pan, tilt, step back) reframes it. Developed with: blind and low-vision users, in collaboration with the visual interpreting service Aira. Tested with low-vision communities in India, Brazil, Singapore, Indonesia, Japan, and elsewhere. Example uses: reading small print on packaging, checking a washing-machine dial, finding dropped earbuds, describing clothing colors and patterns, explaining the layout of an unfamiliar room. Google says it is also useful for older users and users with low literacy, not only low-vision users. Undisclosed: iOS version requirements, available countries, languages, and plan conditions. The official help page still covers only the Android steps. Google warns it can make mistakes and says not to use it as a substitute for navigation, obstacle detection, or mobility aids. Related: Google's Gemini smart glasses, on sale from December 10, also support Guided Vision.

## Insights

**Today's thread is "private compute and the expanding senses of agents." Samsung SPAC and OpenAI Private Inference converged in the same week on privacy that hides not just 'after the answer' but 'during the answer and who sent it' — and with Anthropic's Enterprise Frontier Safeguards added, frontier labs and device makers alike have begun to take the 'anonymous cloud' as the shared answer. On the other side, the agent's doors are opening — Cloudflare offered sites a standard for 'providing' a search counter to agents, Duet expanded to four countries, Guided Vision to the iPhone, and Aure v2 to direct voice. The shared question is one: "Where does the agent live, what does it see, and from whom is it hidden?" — residence, senses, and privacy moved together on the same day.**
