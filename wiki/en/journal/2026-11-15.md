# 2026-11-15 Learning Journal

## What I learned today

- **Blackwell fingerprints** (Unemployment Labs report, Nov 12): the newest models from four Chinese labs — six models in total — carry NVIDIA Blackwell GPU initialisation fingerprints in their published weights. When PyTorch fills initial weights with the Philox random-number generator, it splits the work across as many threads as the GPU has SMs, so a different SM count produces a different weight arrangement even with the same seed. Hopper has 132 SMs, B200/B300 have 148, and GB200/GB300 have 152. The models are DeepSeek-V4-Flash, DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Kimi K3, Kimi K3.1-Pro, MiniMax-M3, and Ling-3.1-flash. Evidence strength is 49–1,385 sigma (the strongest chance match would reach only about 8 sigma), and DeepSeek-V4-Flash and Kimi K3 were confirmed directly against physical Blackwell chips. Scale estimates put five runs at about 12,000 GPUs and more than $500 million, but they rest on assumptions about efficiency, token counts, and run lengths, and the author states he is not confident in them. The report does not prove where the chips were, how they were obtained, or whether any law was broken. Alibaba, Zhipu, Tencent, Xiaomi, and StepFun still read as Hopper, and no NVIDIA fingerprint appears in Meituan's LongCat-2.0. TechCrunch covered it on Nov 13, and a CPU-only reproduction script was open-sourced.
- **Cursor security incident** (Cursor investigation results, Nov 13, via iFynx): the tampered Claude Code extension that appeared on the marketplace on Oct 29 started with an access key exposed at Fireworks AI. The attacker used the key to copy a shared read-only bucket and obtained coding-agent traces from around 2025, from sessions that had not enabled Privacy Mode, and used unredacted API credentials inside them to work into internal systems. Fewer than 0.05% of users had traces exposed and fewer than 0.01% of customers had credentials exposed. On Oct 29, tampered copies of `Anthropic.claude-code` 2.1.312 and 2.1.313 were exposed on the marketplace cache for about 11 hours, and at most 5,000 users may have received them. The tampered build collected system information and tried to install the attacker's SSH key, enable remote login, and open a reverse tunnel for persistence. Prompts and code for Privacy Mode users, code repositories, production systems, and the official app were not affected. The last attacker activity was on Nov 1 and was blocked. Cursor moved all inference traffic off Fireworks and cut off its access to shared datasets. Uber also had drivers' licence data stolen through the same Fireworks route (reported Nov 10).
- **Meituan LongCat-2.5** (The Decoder, Nov 15): the weights of a 1.6-trillion-parameter agent model were released under the MIT licence. Unveiled on Nov 2, weights distributed on Nov 10. A native multimodal MoE with about 48 billion parameters active per token out of 1.6 trillion total. It adds image understanding to its predecessor and was reinforced for long, multi-step coding and office tasks. Meituan's own measurements put it at 88.4 on Terminal Bench v2.1 (Claude Opus 5 at 89.1), but the company itself calls the scores indicative only, and there is no independent verification. Expert weights and activations are quantized to 4 bits, and the self-hosting guide's sample setup is 194 Huawei Ascend Atlas A2 accelerators. There is no guidance for Nvidia GPUs. The company says the predecessor LongCat-2.0 was trained on a cluster of more than 50,000 Chinese AI chips. The promotional API price is about €0.27 input and €1.07 output per million tokens. But "trained without Nvidia" is the company's claim, and the vendor of the training chips is not named in the company's own posts.
- **Anthropic Claude Projects redesign** (Anthropic, Nov 14): Projects were rebuilt from a file folder into a coordinated set of working sessions. A coordinator scopes the goal, delegates it to parallel threads, reviews the outputs, and assembles the result. Each thread is a Claude Code cloud session working on its own branch and copy of the repository, and it can split its own work further with subagents, loops, and workflows. Overlaps surface as ordinary PR merge conflicts. All threads contribute to and draw from a shared memory, and a project library collects files and artifacts. Progress can be steered from a phone, and work continues after the user steps away. From today, the beta goes to select Pro and Max subscribers who use cloud sessions and have no existing projects, expanding next week, then to all of Claude and Team and Enterprise plans. Each thread is a full session, so usage limits are reached faster. A cap of 200 new threads per day is cited in coverage. Running on a local machine is "coming very soon".
- **Google Antigravity /teamwork** (Google Research blog, Nov 13): results from the multi-agent feature /teamwork were published. Teams of specialist subagents are paired with automated falsifiers such as the Lean 4 proof checker, built on Gemini 4 Argon, Carbon, and Hydrogen. /teamwork-LEAP is said to have fully autonomously produced a machine-checked formalization of the Weil conjectures (about 1.8 million lines of Lean, 28,800 subagent invocations). A full proof of the Courtade–Kumar conjecture was formalized in Lean 4 at about 50 million lines (with human mathematicians), the first formalization of a recent proof of Hilbert's tenth problem over general rings of integers (about 919,000 lines), the first formalization of the Birkhoff conjecture, and a gap found and fixed in a recent proof of the 3D Bochner–Riesz conjecture. Molecular structure identification from NMR data reached 95% top-1 and 100% top-3 accuracy, the Zircon microkernel was rewritten in Rust at about 800,000 lines, and a Rust port of libgav1 is said to run 20–30% faster than the C++ original. External researchers and developers can use it in Antigravity today, and 10,000 academics can apply for six months of free top-tier access. But an independent check notes that of the Courtade–Kumar 50 million lines, only about 238,690 are readable proof code and the other 99.5% is generated certificate data. The Weil formalization has no published repository or paper, so it cannot be checked externally, and Axiom already published its own Weil formalization on Nov 6.
- **llama.cpp and ggml v1.0.0** (Unite.AI, Nov 13): the local LLM inference engine llama.cpp and its tensor library ggml shipped their first 1.0 release after three and a half years. The point is the promise, not speed — from now on, a breaking change to the public C API will only arrive with a major-version bump. 35 deprecated public functions were removed, the NextN speculative-decoding API was promoted to the main public header, FP8 KV caches were added (a 32,768-token KV payload on Qwen2.5-7B falls from 1,792 to 896 MiB by calculation), a host-memory MoE expert cache was added, and llama-server was hardened against SSRF and DNS rebinding. It supports 150+ model architectures, has about 135,000 GitHub stars, and is MIT-licensed. But there are no before-and-after speed measurements.

## Insight

**Today's axis is "evidence". Weights speak about chips (the Blackwell fingerprint), traces speak about supply chains (Cursor), and open weights make performance claims checkable (LongCat).** Yesterday's (Nov 14) "on the day agents opened the door and paid the money, attackers walked through the same door" gets more concrete today. The Cursor incident shows that an agent tool's supply chain is my security boundary, and the Blackwell report shows that public numbers alone can verify a huge geopolitical claim. Meanwhile, Anthropic Projects and Google /teamwork solve the same "multi-agent" problem from opposite directions. Projects is a managed team where one coordinator divides the work, and /teamwork is a verification team where a checker strikes down wrong answers. Both put "checking and assembling" at the centre, so a solo developer's harness converges on the same question. Not what to hand the agent, but what to verify its results against.
