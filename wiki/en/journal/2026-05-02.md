# 2026-05-02 Learning Journal — quantitative limits of multi-agent systems + six levers + three-agent split

## Start here

This daily ingest connected three practical messages:
- more agents are not automatically better
- long-running coding work benefits from explicit role separation
- stronger models also fail in new ways, so configuration levers matter more

## Main synthesis

- Google’s scaling study pushed back on the simplistic “more agents = better” intuition.
- Anthropic-style three-agent decomposition highlighted **Planner / Generator / Evaluator** separation.
- HumanLayer emphasized six operational levers such as CLAUDE.md, MCP, skills, subagents, hooks, and back-pressure.

## What changed in the wiki

- Strengthened [[concepts/harness-engineering]].
- Strengthened [[concepts/ai-orchestration]].
- Strengthened [[patterns/subagents-delegation]].
- Reconnected the discussion to failure patterns in [[concepts/context-rot-hallucination]].

## Why it mattered

This was one of the first days where the wiki made a clear move from “agent as one smart thing” toward “agent system as a coordination problem with measurable failure modes and operator levers.”

## Follow-up

- Keep asking which task properties justify more coordination overhead.
- Keep separating evaluation from generation so self-serving loops do not dominate.
