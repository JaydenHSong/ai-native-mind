# 2026-11-21 Learning Journal

## What I learned today

- **Nasdaq drops 5.28% — AI stocks lose $4T in a week, NVIDIA falls 14% on report of SEC accounting probe** (Motley Fool, Reuters, CNBC, 11-20): the Nasdaq fell 5.28% to 22,617.73, erasing its entire 2026 gain (about −2.7% year to date). The S&P 500 fell 3.70% to 6,910.39, a seventh straight decline and a correction, 11.6% below its October 6 all-time high. The Dow fell 1.93% to 48,147.33. For the week, the S&P fell 8.6%, the Nasdaq 10.0%, and the Dow 8.0% — the worst week since March 2020. NVIDIA closed down 14.01% at $125.53, with an intraday low of $124.50, a 52-week low, and down 23% for the week, its worst week since the 2008 financial crisis. About $2.5T in market value erased from the peak (per Motley Fool). Losses deepened late in the session after Reuters reported the SEC is examining NVIDIA's accounting practices — unconfirmed by both the SEC and NVIDIA, and a separate thread from the November 19 California subpoena. AMD down in the low teens, Broadcom and Micron down 12–14%, the Philadelphia semiconductor index −10.7%, Nebius −16.1%, CoreWeave −12.0%. Anthropic closed its second day down 8.5% at $88.91 (intraday low $83.05, within 5 cents of the $83 offering price, about −23% from the day-one high of $115.10). With the greenshoe fully exercised, total proceeds reached about $93.5B, and the IPO closed on November 20. The VIX surged to around 40 (+41%), the 10-year Treasury yield fell below 5%, and Bitcoin broke below $60,000. Background: spreading doubts about AI investment profitability — slowing data-center spending, the way AI projects are financed, signs of spending discipline from big tech, and signals of a hawkish Fed rate stance. Watch the numbers: outlets disagree on the ANT close (Yahoo, Bloomberg, CNN: $88.91; AP, WSJ, MarketWatch: $88.30). The NVIDIA market-value-erased figures also use different baselines (about $2.5T from the peak vs about $669B over six sessions through Nov 19).
- **Trump threatens a 'tremendous tax' on US firms building AI data centers abroad** (Reuters, 11-20): at a White House press conference, President Trump said he would stop US companies from building data centers abroad and impose a "tremendous tax" on them. The remark came right after his return from a visit to China. Targets are Amazon, Google, Microsoft, Meta, and Oracle — building overseas in search of cheaper land, labor, and power, and to avoid permitting fights in the US. No tax rate, legal basis, or timetable was given; imposing a tax requires legislation by Congress. It came on the same day the Nasdaq plunged. Context: together with the November 16 BIS remote-compute rule and the Senate's November 19 permitting-relief bill, it is the latest turn in US policy around where AI infrastructure sits and how it is financed.
- **Amazon v Perplexity hearing — judge skeptical of bid to block Comet shopping agent, discovery stayed** (MLex, Bloomberg Law, 11-20): in a roughly three-hour hearing in San Francisco federal court, Chief Judge Maxine Chesney granted Perplexity's request to stay discovery and expressed deep skepticism about whether Amazon's amended complaint can block Perplexity's Comet AI shopping assistant. Amazon's claim: Comet violated the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA) and California's computer-access law by accessing Amazon's site and customer accounts without authorization, disguising itself as ordinary Chrome, and Perplexity employees testing with their own accounts. The judge's reaction: skepticism about whether the amended claims can overcome the Ninth Circuit's August 2026 ruling vacating the injunction ("It is the user who accesses Amazon's computers"). She did suggest at least one employee-testing claim may survive. No final decision yet. This is the decisive court fight over whether the anti-hacking law can block an AI agent from accessing a website on a user's behalf.
- **Thinking Machines releases Inkling 2 Mini — the open-weights loop transformer that runs on a laptop** (Artificial Analysis, Thinking Machines Lab, 11-20): released as Apache 2.0 open weights. A 26.4B-parameter / 3.4B-active (5.4B effective per token) MoE that loops the middle 12 of its 24 layers three times per token to deepen reasoning without generating more text. About a 20GB checkpoint, 1M-token context, text and image input. Runs on a 32GB Mac or a single DGX Spark. Self-reported performance: Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index 20 (tied with Qwen3.8-27B), DeepSWE v1.1 48.2%, SWE-bench Verified 79.2%, BrowseComp 79.0%, DeepSearchQA 85.3% — claimed leadership among open models under 100B parameters on agentic coding. Cost and speed: about 6x cheaper and about 4x faster per task than Qwen3.8-27B ($0.59 vs $3.53). The larger Inkling 2 Small stays private until next week, pending external safety testing and red-teaming.
- **Google releases Semantic governance policies in preview — every agent tool call checked before it runs** (Google Developers Blog, 11-20): released in preview on the Gemini platform. Through Agent Gateway, an LLM judge checks every tool call an agent proposes before execution. It judges against two things — policies written in plain language by legal and compliance teams, and the user's original intent. A call that does not meet the criteria is replaced with a configurable denial message and never executes; every judgement and its reasoning is recorded in Cloud Logging. Not only Gemini but also Anthropic and OpenAI-compatible models are supported. Positioned alongside IAM, Model Armor, and Agent Anomaly Detection as defence in depth. Indirect prompt injection and social engineering are the explicit targets. Companion announcement: Agent Anomaly Detection (Private Preview) extended to agents outside Google Cloud (AWS Lambda, Microsoft Foundry, and others) via OpenTelemetry traces and logs.
- **Apple is building its own AI agent — job posting deleted, macOS 27.3 makes rival agent approval 14 steps** (TechCrunch, Bloomberg, 11-19–20): per Bloomberg's Mark Gurman, Apple is developing an autonomous AI agent that browses the web, controls apps, and writes and debugs code. The evidence is a 'Senior Engineering Manager, Agentic AI – Proactive' job posting published that week; Apple deleted the posting on November 20. The agent's shape: a proactive, general-purpose agent that can "use a computer the way a capable person does" — web browsing, cross-app work, running commands, writing and debugging code, calling services, and orchestrating specialist sub-agents. It works with the Siri, UI Frameworks, and Apple Foundation Models teams. Launch is targeted for as early as the end of 2027, but development is at an early stage; Apple declined to comment. New CEO John Ternus, who took office September 1, 2026, is pressing software chief Craig Federighi to accelerate development (per Techstrong.ai). Anthropic fell 4.7% in after-hours trading ($92.60) right after the report; Meta fell about 2%. macOS 27.3 beta: consent warnings and approval steps for outside AI agents are being tightened. Splitting Device Control and Data Access settings stretches enabling ChatGPT computer-use support from 4 steps to 14. Rivals read it as protecting Apple's own turf, and a DOJ suit over blocking third-party access is pending. Context: together with Google's Gemini computer-use launch on Mac (Nov 19) and Anthropic's expansion of Claude Computer Use to enterprises (Nov 20), the competitive shape of computer-use agents is forming.

## Insights

**Today's axis is "price, policy, and platform all landing on the same day." The Nasdaq gave back its entire 2026 gain in one day, and at the center of it NVIDIA was hit with an SEC probe report. The same day, the president threatened a tax on overseas data centers, and Apple — while building its own agent — stretched the procedure for turning on a rival agent to 14 steps. The market expressed its doubts about AI as a price, the government as a tax threat, and the platform as friction, each in its own language. Underneath, Google (pre-execution checks) and Thinking Machines (a loop model that runs on a laptop) shipped technology that pushes the agent era forward, and in court a hearing opened on the question of "whose hands" an agent uses to enter a website. Pressure and progress landed on the same day — pointing in different directions, not the same one.**
