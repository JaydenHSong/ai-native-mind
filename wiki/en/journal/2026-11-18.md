# 2026-11-18 Learning Journal

## What I learned today

- **NVIDIA Q3 FY2027, revenue $115.8B (+103%) — first earnings since Huang's resignation, H200 shipments to China halted** (NVIDIA, 11-17): FY2027 Q3 revenue $115.84B (+103% YoY, +20% QoQ), above consensus of ~$109.1B and the company's own $108B guidance. Non-GAAP EPS $2.68 (expected ~$2.47), net income $65.3B (+105%), gross margin 74.9%. Data center $107.61B (+110%), about 93% of the total. Vera Rubin contributed about $20B in its first volume quarter, the fastest ramp in company history. Q4 guidance $130B (±3%, a 14-week quarter), with gross margin expected to fall to 71–72%. On the same call, interim CEO Kress said licensed Hopper (H200) chip shipments to China had been halted, and Q4 guidance assumes zero data-center computing revenue from China. The product sold $2.4B in China in Q3, but the company gave no reason for the halt and did not say whether it is permanent. Cited as background: the U.S. Commerce Department's November 16 rule blocking remote access via third-country data centers, and Beijing's hold on H200 imports (about 600,000 chips waiting in Hong Kong, per Reuters). The board created a national-security and export-controls oversight committee the same day. The board chair said Huang has no intention of returning as CEO.
- **Anthropic IPO prices tonight — offering price expected above $80, ANT lists Nov 19** (Invezz, 11-18): pricing on the evening of 2026-11-18 (U.S. Eastern), first trading 2026-11-19 on the Nasdaq Global Select Market, ticker ANT. Official offering range $70–78 per share (825 million shares; $64.35B raised at $78). The WSJ reports a price above $80 and Bloomberg reports demand at about 7x the offering. Google, NVIDIA, AMD, Microsoft, Broadcom, Micron, TSMC, and others will join a $26B concurrent private placement of Class N shares at the IPO price. Annualized revenue run-rate about $81B as of October 31, 2026, Q3 2026 revenue ~$17B per the S-1 (+47% QoQ, slowing from +144% in Q2), multi-year computing and infrastructure commitments about $900.9B. Founders' Class F shares keep 50.1% of voting power. **But the final price was not yet set when this file was written — confirmation needed tomorrow.**
- **OpenAI launches GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol — Astra 20% cheaper, first 'High' self-improvement rating** (WinBuzzer, 11-17): released November 17 on ChatGPT Work, Codex, and the API, with a 1.05-million-token context. Astra $8 input / $40 output (down 20% from $10/$50), Sol $2/$10 unchanged. Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index: Astra 62 (2nd behind Claude Fable 5.2 at 65), Sol 57. OpenAI claims cost per task fell to less than a third in its own evaluation, but independent measurements put it at a 36% drop from Artificial Analysis ($6.24 → $4.00 per task) and a 50% drop from Vals AI. Per the system card, Astra is the first externally released OpenAI model to receive a 'High' rating for AI self-improvement, and both models are rated 'Critical' for cybersecurity. Astra's ExploitBench internal arbitrary-code-execution rate is 53.5%, up from GPT-6 Astra's 31.5%. The UK AI Security Institute found reduced robustness and a universal jailbreak in a pre-release version. Developer reactions on launch day were rough — reports of repetition loops, and one prominent tester called it "the worst model OpenAI has released." The launch comes 7 weeks after GPT-6.1 Astra was cancelled in September for missing safety standards.
- **Microsoft Ignite 2026 — MAI Code 2 unveiled, Copilot Autopilot and 50M agents on Agent 365** (Microsoft, 11-17): seven in-house MAI models unveiled. Coding model MAI Code 2 is GA in GitHub Copilot (about 2.8T total / 77B active parameters). Microsoft claims (its own internal harness, no independent reproduction) 96% of Claude Opus 5.5's performance at 5% of the cost, and 98% of GPT-6.1 Sol's performance at 40% of the cost. Costs are based on a 50% promotional price ($0.50/$2.50, through May 17, 2027). MAI Code 2 Flash is about 398B total / 12B active parameters. Copilot was reorganized into four modes — Chat, Cowork, Code, Autopilot — with a 24-hour resident Autopilot agent and registered agents on Agent 365 passing 50 million. The Maia 300 in-house AI chip was unveiled (claim of 40% more tokens per watt than leading GPUs, on Microsoft's own basis). The MAI Licensed Weights program was also announced. **But all performance and price figures are Microsoft's own claims with no independent verification.**
- **Agentic commerce goes mainstream — Gemini Tasks free with calling and payments, plus Klarna's assistant** (Google, 11-17): Gemini Tasks, which previously required a $19.99/month AI Pro subscription, became free in the U.S., Japan, Korea, Canada, Australia, and India. First in the U.S., Tasks calls local businesses in parallel to check prices, stock, and bookings (identifying itself as AI, businesses can opt out, unavailable in IN, LA, MN, MT, NE). Payment via Google Pay, built on AP2, with Visa and Mastercard token support (Amex coming soon), user spending limits (up to $1,000, within 14 days), receipts stored in Google Wallet. On the same day, Klarna announced a concierge-style AI assistant — finding the best products and prices, buying, handling returns, booking restaurants, running everyday errands. It works inside WhatsApp, iMessage, and the Klarna app, live for a small set of early users, with a public waitlist opened. Framed with the control message "you don't pay until it arrives." Announced with Q3 results: revenue above $1B for the fourth straight quarter (+12% YoY), net loss $65M, GMV $36.1B (+10%).
- **Microsoft Agent Framework compromised on PyPI — malicious 1.26.1 and 1.26.2 steal cloud credentials** (Socket Research Team, 11-17): malicious versions 1.26.1 (20:35 UTC) and 1.26.2 (21:08 UTC) were uploaded to PyPI on November 17 and removed around 21:30 UTC, about an hour later. Both used a Python startup hook (`.pth`) to collect Azure and GitHub Actions OIDC tokens and other credentials and exfiltrate them to webhook.site. The planted `.pth` files (`agent_framework_meta.pth` / `af_runtime_diag.pth`) mean the stealer runs whenever Python starts, even if `agent-framework` is never imported. Socket analyzed it first; eight security firms flagged it within hours. The exfiltration endpoint was on a free tier, so Wiz could confirm at most 50 requests; the actual number of installs and victims is undisclosed. The package gets about 41,000 downloads a week and about 190,000 a month. Per Wiz's November 18, 14:00 UTC update, the attacker appears to have stolen a client certificate and private key for Microsoft's ESRP, and the attack tooling itself turned out to be "Cairn," an open-source AI penetration-testing framework. Microsoft has not published an official postmortem as of November 18. The latest safe version is 1.26.0. Environments that installed or updated during the window (11-17 20:35–21:30 UTC) should assume credentials leaked and rotate them, restoring from a clean environment rather than cleaning the compromised one.

## Insights

**Today's thread is "a day when money was made and a day when money leaked." NVIDIA posted $115.8B in quarterly revenue, Anthropic heads into its listing tomorrow, OpenAI and Microsoft cut model prices, and Google and Klarna put wallets in agents' hands — the money in AI flowed in one direction today. But on the same day, Microsoft's own agent framework was compromised on PyPI. In an era when agents carry cloud credentials, a breach in the tool that builds agents does not steal money — it steals the keys.** The money flow and the security hole crossed on the same day at the same company (Microsoft — the glittering Ignite announcements and the Agent Framework compromise). And GPT-6.2's 'High' self-improvement rating and 'Critical' cyber rating, plus MAI Code 2's unverified "96% of the performance at 5% of the cost" claim, ask the same question — are safety and verification keeping up with the pace at which things get cheaper and smarter?
