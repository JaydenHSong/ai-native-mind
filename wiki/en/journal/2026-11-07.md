# 2026-11-07 Learning Journal

## What I learned today

- **OpenAI's $11B first close**: Bloomberg reported on 2026-11-06 that OpenAI secured about $11 billion in the first close of a new funding round, at a $1.25 trillion valuation. Reuters, CNBC and TechCrunch confirmed the same figures. The original goal of $30 billion or more at about $1.4 trillion was cut to a $20 billion target, and the first close came in at $11 billion. The valuation is about 47% above the March round ($122 billion at $852 billion). OpenAI has not confirmed it publicly.
- **Meta's Muse Wonder-trained models for all users**: on 2026-11-06 Meta began rolling out specialized models trained with Muse Wonder to all Muse and Meta AI users. The core is Active Memory (keeping context across conversations) and Active Focus (continuing toward a goal despite obstacles). Meta claims a 2.4x lower chance of forgetting critical information, a 67% improvement in self-correcting and completing everyday tasks, and critical-context use up from 76% to 90%. Wonder itself stays for paid subscribers.
- **Anthropic's agent memory "dreaming"**: Anthropic published the engineering blog "The components of agent memory" on 2026-11-06. In offline time between sessions, Claude re-reads its logs, corrects wrong notes and builds a wiki — "dreaming." On CL-Bench, Opus 5.5 rose from 0.655 to 0.838, and a wiki built from Stainless's internal files lifted 25 evaluation questions from 13% to 48% of graded criteria. The design rule: "tell Claude what memory is for, but not how to use it."
- **OpenAI and Ligo's BioBasicsBench**: OpenAI published a 100-task benchmark on 2026-11-06 that tests whether agents can read raw wet-lab data. At the highest reasoning setting, GPT-6 Astra scored 66.7%, GPT-6.1 Sol 62.0% and GPT-5.6 Sol 43.8%. Scores are partial-credit averages, all seven models tested are OpenAI's own, and the dataset was not released with the announcement.
- **DeepMind finds weaknesses in 1024-bit RSA**: Google DeepMind announced on 2026-11-06 that human-supervised AI agents found two weaknesses in legacy 1024-bit RSA — a signature-forgery path and cheaper batch factoring. The 1,600x improvement for the short-digest case is a theoretical estimate; the measured result is 3.5x less CPU time on the benchmark reproduction, and batch factoring cuts per-key cost by about 30x. Modern 2048-bit and larger keys are safe, DeepMind said. The announcement came as an X post and technical papers, with no separate blog post confirmed.
- **NVIDIA Cosmos 3 Nano open weights**: NVIDIA published four Cosmos 3 Nano checkpoints on 2026-11-05 via Hugging Face. All are 16B-parameter models under the OpenMDW1.1 license permitting commercial use, covering driving-data generation, streaming simulation, simulator-to-photorealistic-video and bimanual robot prediction. A catch-up item missed in the previous run.

## Insights

The day's thread is that "agents that remember" arrived the same day in two forms. Anthropic published the internal design of memory (dreaming), and Meta put memory features in front of all users — one side showing how memory works, the other shipping memory to everyone. On the same day, DeepMind showed agents finding weaknesses in old cryptography, and OpenAI published a benchmark measuring whether agents can read real lab data — agents are moving fast into the territory of discovery and judgment. Meanwhile OpenAI's $11 billion first close signals that the funding supporting that speed is no longer as ample as it was. Acceleration and funding, memory and discovery crossed on the same day.
