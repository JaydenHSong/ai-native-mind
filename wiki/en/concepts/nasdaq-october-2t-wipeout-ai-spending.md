# Nasdaq's October $2T Wipeout — AI-Spending Fears and Alphabet's Record One-Day Drop

## TL;DR

Reuters reported October 30, 2026 that more than $2 trillion in market value was set to be wiped from the Nasdaq 100 during October, with the index down over 5.8% for the month. Alphabet suffered its biggest one-day percentage drop on record on October 30 (about $500 billion, −12.6% in related Reuters coverage), and the same day brought OpenAI's funding-target cut and Meta's record fundraise.

## Key facts

- **October's decline**: Nasdaq 100 market value down more than $2T for the month; index down over 5.8% in October, 2.9% on October 30 alone, and 7.8% from its October 6 record close.
- **Alphabet**: its October 30 drop was its largest one-day percentage fall on record (about $500B, −12.6%, per related Reuters coverage).
- **Financing fears**: Meta's record $97.5B fundraise and Microsoft's roughly $25B bond sale (its first US investment-grade sale in 9+ years) heightened dilution and debt concerns.
- **Spending forecasts**: Morgan Stanley forecasts overall AI-infrastructure capex surpassing $1T next year; J.P. Morgan expects $900B in 2026 alone and $5.5T by 2030. The 2026 capex of Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, Oracle, and Microsoft likely equals their cumulative spend since ChatGPT's November 2022 launch.
- **Counterpoint**: the same piece notes Magnificent Seven revenue remains strong, and Microsoft/SpaceX results suggested AI spending is starting to pay off.

## Why it matters

1. **The funding climate is the ecosystem climate**: hyperscaler share prices and bond terms feed directly into API pricing, credits, and partner programs downstream.
2. **Payback is now the question**: against $1T-scale capex forecasts, markets ask when spending converts to revenue. Monetization logic is no longer one lab's problem but the whole market's.
3. **Three signals in one day**: the Nasdaq slide, OpenAI's shrinking round, and Meta's mega-raise landed together. The funding line for the AI buildout is being repriced as a regime, not a headline.

## Limitations

- This is a market-reaction story; the October wipeout figure is an intraday estimate at the time of reporting.
- Alphabet's −12.6% figure comes from related coverage, not the main piece.
- Capex figures are Morgan Stanley and J.P. Morgan forecasts, not confirmed spending.
