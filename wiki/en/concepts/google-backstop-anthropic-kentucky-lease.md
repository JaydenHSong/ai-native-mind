# Google Backstops Anthropic's $19B Kentucky Data Center Lease — TeraWulf 8-K, Google May Take Over the Lease or a Stake if Anthropic Defaults

## Start here

**Analogy**: Anthropic signed a 20-year lease on a very large building, and the landlord worried, "what if you can't pay the rent?" So a wealthy neighbor, Google, wrote a guarantee: "don't worry — if they can't pay, I will. And if they can't pay, I may move into the building myself." The guarantee makes it easier for the landlord to borrow money from the bank.

| Term | Meaning |
|------|------|
| **Credit support agreement** | A contract in which a third party guarantees a tenant's payment obligations — if the tenant cannot pay, the guarantor pays instead |
| **401 MW** | The power scale of the data center — roughly the electricity for 400,000 homes |
| **Project financing** | Borrowing secured against the building and equipment itself — a creditworthy tenant means a lower interest rate |

## One-line definition

On 2026-11-04, TeraWulf disclosed in a Form 8-K that Google signed three credit support agreements (CB-1, CB-2, CB-3) backstopping Anthropic's rent under a 20-year lease for about 401 MW of compute capacity at the Justified Data Campus in Hawesville, Kentucky. Total contracted lease commitments are about $19 billion. If Anthropic fails to pay, Google can take over the lease or pay a termination fee and take a stake in the project subsidiary.

## Key facts

- **Deal structure**: five parties — landlord Raylan Data (a TeraWulf subsidiary), master landlord Justified DataPower, landlord guarantor TeraWulf, tenant Anthropic, and Google. The lease was signed on July 6, 2026 for 20 years, with rent obligations starting when each data hall (CB-1, CB-2, CB-3) is delivered. Capacity delivery is phased from late 2027 through the second quarter of 2028.
- **How the backstop works**: each guarantee takes effect from the commencement date of the final data hall under its lease and ends when Anthropic (or a guaranteeing affiliate) holds investment-grade credit ratings for at least three months. The purpose is to help secure project financing; Alphabet's senior unsecured debt is rated AA+ by S&P and Aa2 by Moody's.
- **Default scenario**: if Anthropic cannot pay rent or becomes insolvent, Google can step in as tenant or, under certain conditions, pay a termination fee in exchange for a proportional ownership stake in the project subsidiary. For the first six years after the support takes effect, Google generally has to take over the lease rather than end it (CoinDesk).
- **Construction**: TeraWulf also signed a definitive engineering, procurement and construction (EPC) agreement with Fluor Enterprises for the three buildings. Project debt is not yet committed, and Fluor's guaranteed maximum price is still being established.

## Why it matters (solo-developer view)

1. **A new pattern in AI infrastructure finance**: instead of the lab borrowing directly, a big tech company guarantees the rent. Anthropic's $895.4 billion in compute commitments (S-1/A) is backed in this way. When asking "who really pays for AI infrastructure," the guarantor has to be part of the answer.
2. **Google takes on Anthropic's risk**: a competitor guaranteeing Anthropic's rent suggests Google gets something in return. One analysis reads section 2.k of the credit support agreement as binding the tenant to Google-approved TPUs only — an unconfirmed reading, but the hypothesis that the price of the guarantee is silicon choice is worth watching.
3. **The default scenario is already in the contract**: the clause letting Google take a stake shows the backstop is not a favor but an option — "if you fail, I take it." It is a concrete example of how risk is shared in AI infrastructure finance.

## Limitations (stated)

- One analysis (Mosaic Singularity) reads section 2.k of the credit support agreement as binding the tenant to Google-approved TPUs for the whole term. This is a single analysis's reading; the 8-K and press release do not mention a TPU condition directly. It needs separate confirmation.
- Project debt is not yet committed and Fluor's maximum price is not yet set. No rent accrues before the data halls are delivered.
