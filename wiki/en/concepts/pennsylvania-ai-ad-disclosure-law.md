# Pennsylvania's AI Ad Disclosure Law — Targeted Labels Only for Misleading Synthetic Ads

## TL;DR

Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro signed the Artificial Intelligence Deceptive Advertising Act (Senate Bill 806, Act 99 of 2026) on 2026-10-22, effective 2026-12-21, 60 days later. The Senate passed it 48–2 on 2026-06-10 and the House 163–40 on 2026-10-07. It is not a blanket label for every AI-assisted ad: disclosure is required only when synthetic content creates a false consumer perception about the nature, origin, or authenticity of the advertised goods or services.

## Key facts

- **Trigger**: the advertiser knowingly, recklessly, or willfully creates or disseminates an ad using synthetic content — images, video, audio, or text significantly modified or generated by AI — in a way that creates a false consumer perception.
- **Disclosure duties**: clear and conspicuous, appearing when the synthetic content is first presented, in the same medium, and where technically feasible permanent or extraordinarily difficult to remove. Format-specific standards cover visual, audio, and print disclosures.
- **Exemptions**: content that is evidently fictional, parody, satire, commentary, or criticism, and content limited to text.
- **Reach**: the law applies to ads aimed at Pennsylvania regardless of where they were produced — an out-of-state ad aired in Pennsylvania carries the obligation, which analysts expect to push compliance toward a national default rather than geofencing.
- **Open questions**: the boundaries of "significantly modified," "evidently fictional," and "false consumer perception" are left open for regulators and courts. A reported $3,000 fine cap comes from a single outlet and is unconfirmed.

## Why it matters

1. **Targeted beats blanket**: labels only when synthetic content deceives — a realistic direction for AI-content labeling design.
2. **State law sets national defaults**: production location is irrelevant, so ad tooling needs a Pennsylvania-aware default, not a state-by-state branch.
3. **"Significantly modified" becomes a product spec**: recording how much AI edited a creative asset, and attaching the label before launch, is becoming a required feature for ad tools.

## Limitations (explicit)

- Key terms are open-textured, so the actual enforcement scope is unknown.
- The $3,000 fine cap is single-source and unconfirmed.
- Reports describing the law as labeling "all AI ads" are inaccurate — it applies only to trigger-meeting ads.
