# Anthropic's Draft US AI Safety Bill — $1B/50-Death Thresholds, Commerce Department-to-Court Shutdown Procedure

## TL;DR

Anthropic is privately lobbying Congress with a self-drafted federal AI safety bill — confirmed by Politico and Reuters on 10/16, re-reported 10/18. Not yet introduced; no sponsor identified. The draft allows a court-ordered model shutdown only on "foreseeable, imminent risk" of $1B+ damage or 50+ deaths/serious injuries, via Commerce Department-registered independent evaluators notifying the Department within 72 hours, followed by a petition to a DC federal judge. Other federal agencies are explicitly barred from independent shutdowns.

## Key facts

- **Status** (10/16 Politico/Reuters, 10/18 re-reports): private lobbying stage. Not introduced, no sponsor identified.
- **Shutdown threshold**: only on "foreseeable, imminent risk" causing **$1B+ damage or 50+ deaths/serious injuries**.
- **Covered risk pathways, limited to three**: bioweapon creation/leak, model escape from developer control, cyberattacks — no shutdown for other reasons.
- **Procedure**: Commerce-registered independent evaluators assess periodically → notify the Department within 72 hours of an imminent risk → the Department petitions a DC federal judge. **Other federal agencies' independent shutdowns explicitly prohibited**.
- **Anthropic spokesperson**: codifies the company's "Advanced AI Framework" — grants "the ability to block or stop dangerous deployments of the most powerful models."

## Why it matters

1. **Self-drafted regulation**: the regulated writing the rules — the draft's numbers ($1B/50/72h) become the starting point for industry standards. "Shutdown capability" turns from design constraint into a hard product requirement.
2. **Kill-switch codification**: the NYC hearing's municipal kill-switch debate moves to federal draft text — the bridge from "voluntary choice" (runtime safety) to "legal obligation."
3. **The centralization paradox**: shutdown authority is unified in Commerce-to-court while barring other agencies — "nobody can shut it down" replaced by "it can only be shut down through a defined procedure." The same question applies to agent governance: who, by what procedure, stops an agent?

## Limitations (explicit)

- Still **a draft under lobbying**, not a bill — passage and final text undetermined.
- Original reporting dates to 10/16 — two-day-old news, collected on 10/18 re-reports.
- The contents of Anthropic's "Advanced AI Framework" are not detailed in press.
