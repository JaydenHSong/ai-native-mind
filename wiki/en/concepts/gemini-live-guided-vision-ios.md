# Gemini Live Guided Vision — Spoken Camera Guidance Expands to iOS

## Start here

**Analogy**: Imagine a friend standing next to a person who cannot see well, saying "there is a washing machine in front of you now, the dial is on the right." Guided Vision is the phone playing that friend's role. Point the camera and it describes what it sees in spoken words — and if the camera is pointed wrong, it tells you how to adjust it: "turn slowly to the right."

| Term | Meaning |
|------|------|
| **Guided Vision** | A Gemini Live feature — it describes what the camera sees in real time in spoken words and guides the camera position by voice |
| **Gemini Live** | Google's real-time voice-and-video conversation AI — share the camera and talk about what it sees |
| **Multimodal** | A method of handling several kinds of information together, such as speech, text, images, and video |

## One-line definition

The iOS expansion of Gemini Live's "Guided Vision", announced by Google's official Gemini account (@GeminiApp) on X on 2026-11-25 — it describes what the camera sees in spoken words and, when the framing is off, guides the camera position by voice. The first platform expansion since the October 1 Android launch.

## Key points

- **How to use**: open Gemini Live and share the camera; Gemini describes what it sees in real time and answers follow-up questions. If the camera is too high, too close, or off-target, voice guidance (pan, tilt, step back) reframes it.
- **Developed with**: blind and low-vision users, in collaboration with the visual interpreting service Aira. Tested with low-vision communities in India, Brazil, Singapore, Indonesia, Japan, and elsewhere.
- **Example uses**: reading small print on packaging, checking a washing-machine dial, finding dropped earbuds, describing clothing colors and patterns, explaining the layout of an unfamiliar room. Google says it is also useful for older users and users with low literacy, not only low-vision users.
- **Undisclosed**: iOS version requirements, available countries, languages, and plan conditions. The official help page still covers only the Android steps. Google warns it can make mistakes and says not to use it as a substitute for navigation, obstacle detection, or mobility aids.
- **Related**: Google's Gemini smart glasses, on sale from December 10, also support Guided Vision.

## Why it matters

1. **The accessibility axis of camera-based real-time multimodal agents**: Gemini Live expands from "conversation partner" to "speaking eyes" — voice agents moving beyond text and speech to handle live camera video. Accessibility features that commercialize first often spread to general users (captions → voice assistants, and so on).
2. **The agent's "seeing" ability in practice**: a feedback loop that corrects the camera position by voice goes beyond simple image description — the agent interacting with the user's physical environment. The mobile version of an axis that continues to robots and wearables.
3. **Stated limits are design information**: Google's warning — "do not use it as a substitute for navigation, obstacle detection, or mobility aids" — sets the scope of responsibility on the assumption that real-time vision can err. For accessibility features, stating what must not be delegated is itself part of product design.
