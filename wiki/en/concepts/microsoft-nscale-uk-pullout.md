# Microsoft Walks Away from Nscale's UK Data Center — First Crack in the AI Neocloud Boom, Days Before a $3B IPO

## TL;DR

The Telegraph's 10/18 exclusive (Reuters re-report): Microsoft terminated its agreement as anchor tenant of British AI-infrastructure firm Nscale's Loughton, Essex data center. Nscale filed for a US IPO last month targeting up to $3B at a reported $35B valuation; Microsoft accounts for ~40% of its contracted revenue. The stated reason is power — the UK grid told Nscale no adequate supply before the 2030s. Norway's flagship site is six months behind (6.4 of 30 MW operating). Context: global AI debt issuance fell from $113B (June) to $23B (September); CoreWeave dropped 25% last week.

## Key facts

- **The termination**: Microsoft was to anchor Nscale's first UK data center (23,000 Nvidia chips planned). Microsoft is one of Nscale's two largest customers, ~40% of contracted revenue.
- **The power problem**: UK grid officials told Nscale the Loughton site would not get adequate power until the 2030s. The site was meant to go live in 2026; March photos showed a scaffolding yard.
- **Norway too**: flagship Norwegian facility six months behind schedule, 6.4 of 30 MW operating; reports of cooling leaks, regulatory hurdles, a fire.
- **Both sides**: Microsoft — "committed to the partnership; this decision relates to a single site." Nscale — "relationship strong and long-term; exploring alternative power and other UK sites."
- **The IPO**: US filing last month; up to $3B raise at a reported $35B valuation; Microsoft and Anthropic named as major customers; Nvidia-backed; competes with CoreWeave, Nebius, Crusoe, Lambda; contracted value $100M → $103B+ in 2.5 years.
- **The funding squeeze**: FT (10/10) — global AI debt issuance $113B (June) → $23B (September). CoreWeave −25% last week (steepest weekly drop since November). Australia's Firmus abandoned its IPO.
- **Still on track**: other sites under the September 2025 $24B Microsoft contract (Texas, Portugal, second Norway facility).

## Why it matters

1. **The bottleneck moves from chips to power**: grid connection, not GPU supply, is now the risk metric for data-center investment — and a cost-pressure signal for cloud pricing in power-constrained regions.
2. **Neocloud risk, quantified**: borrow-to-build plus dependence on hyperscaler tenants — one tenant's exit shakes an IPO. The first large case putting numbers on the gap between "infinite AI demand" and execution risk.
3. **Microsoft's two-sided strategy**: own silicon (Maia 300, 96 chips per rack, 1M units next year) alongside external neocloud dependence — infrastructure diversification as practiced, not preached.

## Limitations (explicit)

- The withdrawal reason is undisclosed by both sides — power is the reported surface reason, not confirmed.
- The $35B valuation and $3B raise are Telegraph-reported figures, not finalized IPO terms.
- Read as part of a trend (with the 10/16 "AI debt boom cracking" story), not as an isolated event.
