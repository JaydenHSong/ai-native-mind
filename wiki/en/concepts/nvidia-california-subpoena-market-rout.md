# California Subpoenas NVIDIA as the Market Routs — NVDA Falls for a Sixth Straight Session

## Start here

**Analogy**: a week after the founder-CEO stepped down, California's attorney general sent the company a subpoena: "hand over the records on whether chips leaked to China and whether investors were misled." The stock fell 3.3% more that day — a sixth straight decline — and the whole U.S. market dropped sharply. Earnings were record-breaking, yet the stock would not rise: the market is now watching the investigation, not the results.

| Term | Meaning |
|------|------|
| **Investigative subpoena** | A document from prosecutors or investigators demanding records — an investigation step, not an indictment (a formal charge) |
| **Export controls** | Government rules barring advanced technology from being sold to certain countries |
| **VIX** | The market's fear index — when it rises, investors are nervous |
| **Short position** | An investment that makes money when a stock falls — a short report is a critical report published by such an investor |

## One-line definition

On November 19, 2026, California Attorney General Rob Bonta issued an investigative subpoena to NVIDIA, examining whether advanced chips and confidential information reached China and whether NVIDIA and former CEO Jensen Huang misled investors. The same day NVDA closed at **$145.98 (−3.3%)**, its **sixth straight daily decline**, while the broader U.S. market fell sharply, the Dow down 1,101 points.

## Key points

- **The subpoena**: Bonta's office issued the investigative subpoena on November 19. It covers (1) whether advanced chips and confidential information flowed to China, and (2) whether investors and the public were misled. A subpoena is an investigation step, not an indictment. The background: Huang acknowledged a relationship with Alice Huang (linked to Megaspeed) and resigned on November 12; the WSJ reported an SEC investigation (Nov 18); the board opened its own investigation and created an export-controls oversight committee. Huang is scheduled to testify before three House committees on December 1.
- **The stock**: NVDA closed November 19 at $145.98 (−3.3%), a sixth straight decline (−16% over six sessions, about $669B in market value wiped out). Down about 32% since October 28 and about −38–40% from the all-time closing high of $239.24 on October 6. It traded near its 52-week low of $145.71 intraday. Premarket on November 20 fell about another 2.6%. Moody's cut NVIDIA's credit outlook from Positive to Stable.
- **Scale of China chip approvals**: per the NYT, NVIDIA has applied for approval to sell more than 40,000 H200 chips to Megaspeed-linked companies. Per House Foreign Affairs Committee documents, the U.S. Commerce Department approved sales of about 879,868 NVIDIA and AMD AI chips to Chinese companies between December 2025 and May 2026 (actual shipments unknown).
- **ARK sells**: ARK Invest sold another 33,430 shares (about $5M) on November 19, after selling 543,092 shares (about $84.8M) on November 16.
- **The broader market**: November 19 — Dow −1,101 points (−2.2%, 49,097.06, below 50,000 for the first time since early June), S&P 500 −2.0% (7,175.83, a sixth straight decline), Nasdaq −2.3% (23,879.20), VIX +14%. AI stocks that day: Nebius −9.4%, CoreWeave −6.1%, Amazon −3.9–4.06%, Meta −3.3%, Alphabet −3.3%, Microsoft −1.87–2.0%, AMD −7.7%. The Nasdaq is about −13.6–14% from its October 6 all-time high.
- **AMD short report**: AMD's −7.7% coincided with short seller Hunterbrook disclosing a short position and publishing a report claiming AMD firmware connects to IEIT Systems, a blacklisted Chinese server vendor in the U.S. The report comes from a party holding a short position, so a conflict of interest must be kept in mind.
- **Acquisition report**: separately, NVIDIA is reported to be in late-stage talks to acquire AI company Inflection (over $500M, per The Information).

## Why it matters (solo-developer lens)

1. **Investigations, not earnings, now move the stock**: Q3 revenue of $115.8B (+103%) on November 17 — a record — did not lift the shares ([[concepts/nvidia-q3-earnings-h200-china]]). The market is asking "what else will come out," not "how much did it earn."
2. **Geopolitics has become company risk**: export controls, alleged China leaks, and a December 1 House testimony now run in one line. Read with [[concepts/china-ai-export-controls]]: for a chipmaker, "where do you sell" is now a legal question, not a technical one.
3. **The second axis of the AI-stock slide**: on top of the debt and overheating worries in [[concepts/ai-debt-boom-cracking]], the same day brought Anthropic's IPO and Intuit's Credit Karma outlook cut. An infrastructure-side investigation and an application-side revenue squeeze were priced in on the same day.

## Limits (stated explicitly)

- A subpoena is an investigation step, not an indictment. "China leaks" and "misleading investors" are the subjects under investigation, not established facts.
- The Commerce Department's 879,868 chip approvals are approvals, not actual shipments.
- The Hunterbrook report comes from a party holding a short position — read it with its conflict of interest in mind.
- "Alice Huang's intelligence-service connection" is a U.S. authorities' assessment, not an officially confirmed fact (see [[concepts/nvidia-huang-ousted-ceo]]).
- confidence is medium — Barron's, Reuters, and TipRanks cross-check, but the investigation is at an early, fluid stage.
