# China's Fast-Tracked Comprehensive AI Law — Full-Lifecycle Regulation Previewed

## Start here

**Analogy**: until now, rules arrived piecemeal, app by app and content type by content type. Now China says it will bind AI from birth to retirement in a single framework law. Only the table of contents and direction are out; the bill itself is not.

| Term | Meaning |
|------|------|
| **NDRC** | China's National Development and Reform Commission, the top economic planning body |
| **Full lifecycle** | The whole span from development and training through deployment, operation and retirement |

## One-line definition

Announced 2026-11-03: China's NDRC said it will accelerate a comprehensive framework AI law covering the full lifecycle, previewing developer and operator duties to disclose safety risks and report incidents and vulnerabilities.

## Key facts

- **Announcement context**: the first in a series of NDRC briefings on the 15th Five-Year Plan (2026–2030). The law is meant to be "basic and comprehensive", forming the foundation for all other AI regulation.
- **Previewed duties**: developers and operators would disclose safety risks and report incidents and vulnerabilities, while regulators set safety standards and ethical guidelines. Rules on data, compute and algorithms — the common resources — and on key application areas would be expedited alongside.
- **Named targets**: frontier developers including DeepSeek, Moonshot AI (Kimi) and Alibaba (Qwen) were cited.
- **Accumulated history**: interim measures for generative AI services (August 2023), mandatory labeling of generated content (September 2025), tiered controls on agent autonomy (from July 15, 2026), the State Council's first mention of a comprehensive AI law in its 2026 legislative plan (May 2026), and the TC260 'AI Security Governance Framework' third edition (September 2026, advisory).
- **Undecided**: no adoption timeline was announced, and detailed obligations will follow in later rules.

## Why it matters (solo-developer view)

1. **Market-entry preconditions may be rewritten at once**: a move from ministry-level measures to a framework law can re-cut the compliance checklist for selling models or agents into China.
2. **Incident reporting becomes a design requirement**: once reporting is law, logging, tracing and incident response stop being add-ons and become launch conditions.
3. **One more regulatory axis**: alongside the US, EU, Australian and Indian frameworks, China's frame means the same model may need different safety and disclosure standards per region.

## Limitations (stated)

- This is an announcement stage with no bill text or adoption timeline, so the strength and scope of duties cannot be inferred.
- Coverage relies on Bloomberg reporting via secondary summaries.
- The ROME crypto-mining incident was cited as background, not as a confirmed cause of the bill.
