# Platforms Blocking AI Agents — Yelp, Delta, and Banks Start Turning Agents Away

## TL;DR

The Verge (Emma Roth, 10/17) reported that Yelp, Delta, JPMorgan Chase, Charles Schwab, Cars.com, and Amtrak are restricting AI agents from OpenAI, Meta, xAI, and others. Yelp allows agents only through a paid data-licensing program; Delta has no partnership enabling third-party agent booking; JPMorgan and Schwab block credential-using agents from customer accounts. Cross-reported by mylistingo, techbuzz.ai, RR Tech Pulse, and Inshorts on 10/17–18.

## Key facts

- **Yelp**: agents get access only through a **paid data-licensing program** — confirmed via TechCrunch. Unlicensed access denied.
- **Delta**: no current partnership or integration enabling third-party agents to shop or book flights; "continuing to evaluate." Steps taken against unauthorized automated activity. Not a formal ban (softer wording).
- **Charles Schwab CEO Rick Wurster** (10/15 investor call): an agent logging in on a client's behalf "is not safe. We are not going to allow that."
- **JPMorgan**: Jamie Dimon (10/13 earnings call) — "not going to allow just any… outside agent to come inside the company, because it's just too risky for clients and for JPMorgan." Spokesperson: outside AI agents blocked from performing customer tasks.
- **Amazon vs Perplexity**: a court first ordered Perplexity to stop agent purchases, an appeals court overturned that in August, Amazon filed an amended suit. The jurisprudence is swinging.
- **Meta's counter**: "turning away a personal agent means turning away the customer behind it." Partnerships with Walmart, Gap, Sephora, Best Buy — Muse can purchase there. Shopify opened its network.

## Why it matters

1. **The next chapter of "whose side is the agent on"**: when steering fails, platforms escalate to **locking the door**. Steering → blocking.
2. **Authority models compressed in practice**: the 5-level LEA framework compresses in reality to "banks: unauthorized actors." Poppy's view-only/change negotiation becomes the only legitimate channel.
3. **Business map and risk**: building an agent that routes around blocked sites risks ToS violations; the platforms' "official agent channels" (licenses, partnerships) become the new distribution.

## Limitations (explicit)

- Amtrak/Cars.com blocking is based on user reports — unverified (noted by RR Tech Pulse).
- Delta's wording is "no partnership, continuing to evaluate," not a formal ban — open to interpretation.
- The Verge original URL was not captured verbatim — cross-verified via mylistingo, techbuzz.ai, and Inshorts.
