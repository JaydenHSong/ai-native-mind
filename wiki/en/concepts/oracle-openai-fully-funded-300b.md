# Oracle Declares OpenAI Infrastructure Deal 'Fully Funded' — 5-Year $300B, 4.5 GW, Shares Down 8.5%

## Start here

**Analogy**: a construction company that won a giant contract announced "we have all the money lined up" — and its share price fell 8.5% anyway. What investors saw was not "the money is lined up" but "how much debt it took to line it up." After selling about $10 billion in stock and $10 billion in bonds in a month, the company also hit the limit on how much more it can raise.

| Term | Meaning |
|------|------|
| **Hyperscaler** | A company operating data centers at very large scale — a big player in cloud infrastructure |
| **Vendor financing** | Funding arranged so the seller (here, Oracle) pre-arranges money for the buyer's (OpenAI's) purchases |
| **52-week closing low** | The lowest end-of-day price in the past year |
| **Free cash flow** | Cash left after all necessary spending — turning positive means the business can run without new borrowing |

## One-line definition

Oracle announced in an X post on November 20, 2026 that financing for its OpenAI infrastructure deal is complete. The deal covers about $300 billion of compute capacity over five years and 4.5 GW of additional data-center capacity in the United States. But Oracle shares fell 8.48% the same day to a 52-week closing low of $101.19 — the market reacted to the debt load, not to the financing being done.

## Key points

- **The declaration**: "financing disclosed at Investor Day is secured; further vendor discussions are optional," calling itself "the first hyperscaler fully funded for this investment cycle."
- **Deal size**: about $300B of compute capacity over five years, plus 4.5 GW of additional US data-center capacity (beyond the Abilene Stargate site).
- **About $20B raised in November**: about $10B in stock (86,206,897 shares at $116 each) plus about $10B in bonds (maturities 2029–2046, rates 7–8.65%). This raise exhausted the $20B cap on additional capital-markets fundraising for fiscal 2027–2030.
- **Progress**: the Abilene campus is nearly complete, the next GW-scale campus in Shackelford County is permitted, and Nvidia Vera Rubin racks are about to be delivered. Free cash flow is targeted to turn positive in the second half of fiscal 2028, and above $30B in fiscal 2029.
- **Market reaction**: ORCL fell 8.48% on the day to $101.19, a 52-week closing low — read as the market reacting to the debt burden and AI-spending skepticism rather than to the completed financing.

## Why it matters (solo-developer lens)

1. **"The money is raised" and "there is money left" are different statements**: the $20B raise consumed the additional fundraising cap for fiscal 2027–2030. It sits on the same line as the diagnosis in [[concepts/ai-debt-boom-cracking]] — "capital could break first" — now evidenced as "the money was raised, but the headroom is gone."
2. **The market looks at the balance sheet, not the contract**: the share price hitting a 52-week low on the day of a $300B contract announcement means investors looked at the debt first. An infrastructure vendor's credit risk is becoming your service's supply-chain risk.
3. **Stargate's funding structure is now visible**: $20B raised through high-yield bonds at 7–8.65% plus a large stock issue — hard evidence that AI infrastructure funding runs on vendor financing and a debt cycle. It is the follow-through of the $20B raise plan in [[concepts/oracle-20b-raise-ai-spending-selloff]] from early November.

## Limits (stated explicitly)

- The fully-funded declaration comes from Oracle's own X post — not independently verified accounting figures.
- The reading of the share move ("reacting to the debt burden") is market commentary, not established causation.
