# Anthropic's Pentagon Rehearing Petition — the Retaliation Fight over 'Supply-Chain Risk' Designation

## Start here

**Analogy**: the government cut off your customers calling your technology "a risk," and you petitioned the full court to "look at it again" — arguing the designation was retaliation for refusing surveillance and kill-weapon work and for speaking out on safety.

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Supply-chain risk designation** | A DoD mechanism branding specific tech or vendors as "supply-chain risks," excluding them from defense contracts |
| **En banc rehearing** | The full appeals court re-hearing a case — a procedure that can overturn a three-judge panel's ruling |
| **41 U.S.C. §4713** | The Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act of 2018 — the legal basis of this case |

## One-line definition

Anthropic's 2026-10-13 petition for **en banc rehearing before the D.C. Circuit** over the Pentagon's **'supply-chain risk' designation** — an attempt to reverse the 9/25 2–1 panel ruling (upholding the DoD's March 2026 designation, blocking Pentagon contractors' Claude use for Pentagon work) as a ruling that "ignored evidence of retaliation."

## Key content

### The petition (2026-10-13, CNBC original)

- Target: the 9/25 **2–1 panel ruling** — upheld the DoD's March 2026 supply-chain-risk designation, blocking defense contractors from using Claude for Pentagon work.
- Counsel: **WilmerHale**. Argument: the panel ignored evidence that the designation was **retaliation for refusing to enable mass surveillance and lethal autonomous weapons and for public AI-safety statements**.
- Key law: **Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act of 2018 (41 U.S.C. §4713)**.

### The parallel front

- **Separate case in San Francisco federal court**: a judge ruled in August that the DoD had violated Anthropic's **First Amendment rights** — Anthropic's position won on the free-expression axis.
- Background: Trump's 2/27 order to stop using Anthropic tech → a **$200M Pentagon contract win** in July 2025 followed by failed negotiations → the March 2026 supply-chain-risk designation.

## Why it matters

1. **The government–lab relationship goes legal**: "safety-advocating labs vs a government waving national security" moves into courtroom combat. The opposite of [[concepts/white-house-ai-accord]]'s "voluntary accord" frame — a different hand of the same government (DoD) brings forced exclusion.
2. **The retaliation claim's fallout**: if "designated because we refused surveillance and kill-weapon work" is accepted as fact, a lab's **public safety principles become a government-contract risk** — a precedent shaking labs' incentives to speak out.
3. **The AI-application precedent for supply-chain law**: the first major case applying a 2018 law (originally for telecom equipment) to a frontier AI model — the outcome reshapes other labs' (OpenAI, Google, etc.) access terms for defense work.
