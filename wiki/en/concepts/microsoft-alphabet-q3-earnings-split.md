# Microsoft and Alphabet Q3 Earnings Split: Azure +48% With Cash Flow vs Cloud Doubled With Negative Cash Flow

## TL;DR

On 2026-10-28, after the US market close, Microsoft and Alphabet reported on the same night and the market split them: Microsoft beat on revenue, EPS, Azure, and free cash flow and rose about 4% after hours, while Alphabet missed on revenue and EPS and fell about 10% after hours despite Google Cloud growing 103%. Both companies said AI demand exceeds available capacity; the market judged how each company is paying for that capacity.

## Key facts

- **Microsoft (FY27 Q1, quarter ended September)**: revenue $92.3B (+19% year over year, above the $90.7B consensus), adjusted EPS $4.89 (above $4.73 expected), net income $36.6B. Azure revenue $33.17B (+48%), disclosed in dollars for the first time. Microsoft Cloud revenue $63.9B (+30%), operating income $45.9B. Capital spending was $39.4B ($45.2B including finance leases), below feared levels, and free cash flow was $18.6B, well above the $8.54B analysts expected.
- **Alphabet (Q3, quarter ended September)**: revenue $127.6B (+25%, below the $128.2B consensus), adjusted EPS $2.67 (below $3.02 expected), GAAP EPS $2.12 (down from $2.87 a year earlier after an $8.7B loss on equity securities swung other income to -$8.2B). Search revenue $63.4B and YouTube advertising $11.2B also missed estimates.
- **The cloud paradox**: Google Cloud revenue $30.71B (+103%) and cloud operating profit $11.1B (from $3.6B a year earlier) still could not offset quarterly capital spending of $51.4B, which pushed free cash flow to -$9.07B — the second straight negative quarter. Alphabet issued $28.7B in senior notes, sold $12.4B of stock, and suspended buybacks for the quarter.
- **Forward spending**: Alphabet held its 2026 capital spending guide at $195–205B and said 2027 spending would rise "significantly." Google said AI Mode searches monetize less than regular search so far; Search grew about 12%. Gemini app users reached 1B, and first-party model APIs ran at about 32B tokens per minute.

## Why it matters

1. **A leading indicator for cloud and API prices**: Azure and Google Cloud growth rates and free cash flow shape the price and capacity of the APIs and GPUs solo developers buy. A cash-flow-negative provider has a stronger incentive to raise prices or ration capacity.
2. **How to verify demand claims**: both companies claimed demand exceeds supply, but the market verified that claim against free cash flow. Reading the cash flow statement beats taking a vendor's demand language at face value.
3. **The case for multi-cloud**: one cloud's financial pressure can become another's opportunity or price move. Single-vendor dependence weakens negotiating leverage.

## Limitations

- After-hours moves (+4%/-10%) are immediate reactions and can change in later trading.
- Microsoft capital spending is reported under two definitions ($39.4B vs $45.2B including leases), so comparisons need a consistent basis.
- The AI Mode monetization remark is a management comment without a published figure.
