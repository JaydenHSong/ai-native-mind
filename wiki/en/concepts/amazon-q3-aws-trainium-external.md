# Amazon Q3 — AWS Accelerates 39%, Trainium Goes on Sale Externally in Late 2027

## TL;DR

Amazon reported Q3 2026 results on October 29 after the close: revenue $201.9 billion (+12%) and EPS $1.89, both slightly below consensus, but AWS grew 39.3% — its fastest in nearly five years — and the operating margin hit a record 13.9%. On the same earnings call, CEO Andy Jassy said Amazon will sell Trainium chips to customers outside AWS starting in late 2027, its first direct hardware competition with Nvidia.

## Key facts

- **Group**: revenue $201.9B (+12%), EPS $1.89 (vs $1.96 expected). Operating income $28.1B (+61%) at a record 13.9% margin. Net income $20.7B, slightly down year over year, against a prior-year quarter that included a $9.5B Anthropic valuation gain.
- **AWS**: revenue $46.0B (+39.3%, accelerating for a sixth straight quarter), operating income $17.8B at a 38.7% margin. A $184B annualized run rate and a $578B backlog, more than double a year earlier. AWS produced about 63% of operating income on about 23% of sales.
- **Outlook**: Q4 revenue guidance $240–247B, operating income $28.5–33.5B. Jassy projects AWS growth above 40% in both 2027 and 2028, says 2027 is largely sold out, and holds 2026 cash capex guidance at about $220B with "meaningfully more" planned for 2027.
- **Trainium goes merchant**: from late 2027, Amazon will sell physical Trainium racks to third-party data centers. Until then Trainium is AWS-only (rented compute, not sold chips). Revenue commitments for Graviton and Trainium each rose more than 20x year over year, and most of Bedrock already runs on Trainium. Jassy also stated the trade-off frankly: external sales realize revenue sooner, but internal use drives more absolute revenue and free cash flow over five to six years.
- **Market reaction**: shares fell after hours despite the AWS acceleration, with reported drops ranging from about 6% to 11% depending on the outlet's measurement point. Cash flow and spending guidance, not headline growth, set the verdict.

## Why it matters

1. **Chips become a product line**: a cloud operator selling its own silicon externally cracks Nvidia's merchant-chip position, in the same direction as Google's TPU external sales.
2. **The vertical-integration trade-off, stated openly**: sell now vs use internally for more later. Jassy's public calculation is a rare visible pricing of infrastructure strategy.
3. **Read earnings by segment**: a revenue miss and a record margin coexisted in one quarter. Segment cash flow, backlog, and guidance — not one headline — decide the read.

## Limitations

- The exact Q3 cash capex figure varies across outlets (about $75.9B vs $77.3B) and is omitted here; only the full-year guidance (about $220B) is used.
- The after-hours share drop is reported as a range because outlets measured at different points.
- Trainium external sales are a late-2027 plan; scale, pricing, and customers are undisclosed.
