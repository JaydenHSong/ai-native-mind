# Microsoft Offers Buyouts to About 60,000 Employees to Fund AI Spending

## TL;DR

On 2026-10-26 Microsoft sent voluntary exit offers to about 60,000 employees — over 27% of its 223,000-person workforce — in the US, UK, Canada, and India. The company expects only 3–5% to accept. The move lands two days before quarterly earnings, against a $175B calendar-2026 capex forecast.

## Key facts

- **Scope**: about 60,000 employees at level 70 and below (vice president and below). Eligible teams include AI experiences, Copilot, CoreAI, Windows + Devices, Xbox, finance, and HR. Marketing, commercial business, security, data centers, and parts of sales are excluded.
- **Company framing**: Chief People Officer Amy Coleman's memo presents the offer as a choice for employees considering a future outside the company and as flexibility for participating organizations to adapt staffing to changing needs, varying by business priority, customer commitments, and workforce needs.
- **Expected uptake**: the company expects 3–5% acceptance (under 3,000 people), per the Seattle Times. An early-2026 program covered about 9,000 US employees, and a separate layoff of about 4,800 followed.
- **Spending backdrop**: calendar-2026 capex forecast $175B; FY2026 capex $115.95B; 223,000 full-time employees at 2026-06-30. Capex per employee rose from $114k in FY2021 to $520k in FY2026 (about 4.6x) — money moving from people to data centers and chips.
- **Market reaction**: shares rose about 1% on the day (about +10% year to date), market cap about $3.90T. The 2026-10-28 earnings call is the checkpoint for whether AI spending converts into productivity and margin.

## Why it matters

1. **Workforce as a funding source**: not only model prices and capital raises but headcount budgets are being reallocated to AI infrastructure.
2. **Headline vs effect**: 60,000 offers against a 3–5% expected acceptance — the gap between the announced number and the real outcome is the story.
3. **Earnings as verdict**: two days later, results test whether the restructuring is justified by AI returns.

## Limitations (explicit)

- Actual acceptances, costs, and any follow-up layoffs are unconfirmed, and reporting notes the memo implies later layoffs remain possible.
- Excluded-department lists differ slightly across sources.
- The $175B figure is a calendar-year forecast while $115.95B is a fiscal-year actual — they are not directly comparable.
