# Anthropic's Jack Clark on 2030 US GDP of $44T — Citing an Internal Economic Model's 'Extreme Scenario'

## TL;DR

At the IMF–World Bank annual meetings in Bangkok on 2026-10-17 (Per Jacobsson Foundation Lecture), Anthropic co-founder and policy head Jack Clark cited the company's internal economic model, saying US GDP could reach ~$44 trillion by 2030 (from over $30T now). He acknowledged the figure "sounds very outlandish." The $44T figure is the model's extreme scenario (+32.4%), not an official forecast — reported by Reuters (10/17), with re-reports by Seoul Economic Daily and Crypto Briefing.

## Key facts

- **Remark**: "US GDP could reach about $44 trillion by 2030," per Anthropic's internal economic model; Clark: "this number sounds very outlandish."
- **Condition**: sustained AI progress, with new industries and inventions accompanying growth at that scale.
- **Context**: Clark called for "unusual and serious rules" and international cooperation.
- **Model structure** (per independent coverage): base scenario $34.1T / transformative-change $36.3T / **extreme $44.4T** (2025 fixed prices) — the $44T is the upper tail, not a consensus forecast.
- **Date caveat**: the Reuters original (2026-10-17 09:14 UTC) precedes the collection window start (14:00 UTC) by ~5 hours. The speech date and re-reports (Seoul Economic Daily 10/18, Crypto Briefing 10/17) fall inside the window.

## Why it matters

1. **Narrator change**: GDP forecasts were central banks' and the IMF's domain. A frontier lab showing up at a macroeconomics conference with an internal model marks technology authorship of the economic narrative.
2. **The politics of extremes**: $44.4T is a "possible upper tail," but headlines run "44T" — the number functions as policy leverage ("rules are needed"), not as a forecast.
3. **Paired with the IMF report**: the same week's IMF Asia labor outlook (measured −10%) versus Clark's scenario-modeled +44T growth — measured data and scenario modeling on the same table is the 2026 landscape.

## Limitations (explicit)

- $44T is the **extreme scenario**, not a consensus forecast — "Anthropic forecasts $44T" is a misreading.
- The internal model's assumptions and methodology are undisclosed — not verifiable.
- The Reuters original sits just outside the window start — an edge case by strict date criteria.
