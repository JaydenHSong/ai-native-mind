# NVIDIA Q3 FY2027: Revenue $115.8B (+103%) — First Earnings Since Huang's Ouster, H200 Shipments to China Halted

## Start here

**Analogy**: a company selling computer chips sold $115.8 billion worth in one quarter (three months). About 93% of that was chips for the giant computer halls (data centers) where AI is trained and run. But on the same day, it said it had stopped shipping an older chip (the H200) to China — and its forecast for next quarter assumes zero revenue from China at all.

| Term | Meaning |
|------|------|
| **Data center** | A giant building full of computers that run AI |
| **Gross margin** | The share of revenue left after production costs — the higher, the more the company keeps |
| **Guidance** | A company's own advance forecast for next quarter's revenue |
| **H200 (Hopper)** | NVIDIA's previous-generation AI chip, sold to China under license |
| **Vera Rubin** | NVIDIA's newest AI chip platform, shipping in volume for the first time this quarter |
| **YoY** | Growth versus the same quarter last year |

## One-line definition

NVIDIA posted quarterly revenue of **$115.8B (+103% YoY)** in its first earnings since founder-CEO Jensen Huang resigned (November 12), and on the same earnings call interim CEO Colette Kress said shipments of licensed Hopper (H200) chips to China had been **halted**. Q4 guidance of $130B assumes **zero** data-center computing revenue from China.

## Key points

- **Earnings**: reported 2026-11-17 after market close, FY2027 Q3 (ended October 25). Revenue $115.84B (+103% YoY, +20% QoQ), above consensus of ~$109.1B and the company's own $108B guidance. Non-GAAP EPS $2.68 (expected ~$2.47), net income $65.3B (+105%). Gross margin 74.9%.
- **Data center**: revenue $107.61B (+110% YoY), about 93% of the total. Hyperscalers $60.1B (+71%), AI cloud, industry, and enterprise $47.5B (+195%). Edge computing $8.2B (+42%).
- **Vera Rubin**: contributed about $20B in its first volume-shipment quarter, the fastest ramp in company history. NVL72 racks are running at CoreWeave, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure, Oracle, and Nebius.
- **Q4 guidance**: revenue $130B (±3%, a 14-week quarter), above consensus of ~$125B. Gross margin is expected to fall to 71–72%. The FY28 revenue-growth outlook was also raised from ~70% to ~75%.
- **China**: interim CEO Kress said shipments of "licensed Hopper data-center products" to China had been halted. The product sold $2.4B in China in Q3, but Q4 guidance assumes zero data-center computing revenue from China. The company gave no reason and did not say whether the halt is permanent. Cited as background: the U.S. Commerce Department's November 16 rule blocking remote access via third-country data centers, and Beijing's hold on H200 imports (about 600,000 chips waiting in Hong Kong, per Reuters). The board created a new national-security and export-controls oversight committee the same day.
- **Leadership**: the first earnings since Huang's resignation, led on the call by interim CEO Kress (also CFO). The board chair said Huang has no intention of returning as CEO.

## Why it matters (solo-developer lens)

1. **Demand has not bent**: revenue more than doubled year over year despite the shock of a founder-CEO ouster. AI infrastructure demand is bigger than "one person's event" at one company. Rubin adding $20B in its first quarter means the product transition is going smoothly.
2. **Zero from China is a structural break**: a product that sold $2.4B in China in Q3 disappears entirely from Q4 guidance. Together with the NDAA's three export-control bills ([[concepts/us-chip-export-controls-ndaa]]), export controls are now showing up as hard numbers in earnings guidance.
3. **Watch the margin**: revenue is growing, but the company itself guided gross margin down to 71–72%. Rubin ramp costs are rising, so "how much it sells" has to be read alongside "how much it keeps."
4. **What to confirm next**: whether the H200 halt is permanent, what happens to the roughly 600,000 chips waiting in Hong Kong, and what the new national-security and export-controls committee actually changes.

## Limits (stated explicitly)

- Earnings figures are NVIDIA's own reporting (primary source), but the roughly 600,000 chips waiting in Hong Kong and Beijing's import hold come via Reuters reporting and the original was not checked directly.
- The company did not state the reason for the H200 halt or whether it is permanent — do not speculate.
- confidence is medium — earnings figures are cross-checked against consensus beats, but the China background comes via press reports and the duration of the halt is unconfirmed.
