# DHS DeepSeek Federal Ban Formalized — Supply-Chain-Based Model Removal Order

## TL;DR

DHS Exclusion and Removal Order #2026-01: US federal civilian agencies are barred from procuring DeepSeek products and ordered to remove them from existing IT systems. Scope covers Hangzhou DeepSeek AI Co., Ltd and affiliates **plus third-party products/services built on DeepSeek technology**. Issued 2026-07-24 (signed by Secretary Markwayne Mullin); published in the Federal Register on 10-20 (FR Doc. 2026-21447, docket DHS-2026-0628) after a 60-day exception window. Legal basis: the 2018 Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act (FASCA). DHS cites "multiple threat vectors" — potential exfiltration of US government user data to China and malware/vulnerabilities in the models themselves. China's foreign ministry (Mao Ning, 10-20) protested "overstretching the concept of national security." Verified via Reuters, TechCrunch, and BleepingComputer.

## Key facts

- **The order**: DHS Exclusion and Removal Order #2026-01, applying to federal civilian executive-branch agencies — procurement ban plus removal from existing systems.
- **Scope**: Hangzhou DeepSeek AI Co., Ltd and affiliates, and third-party products/services built on DeepSeek technology — derivative products, not just direct model use.
- **Timeline**: issued 2026-07-24 → 60-day exception-request window → Federal Register publication 10-20. No removal deadline specified.
- **Legal basis**: the 2018 Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act (FASCA).
- **DHS rationale**: "multiple threat vectors" — potential exfiltration of sensitive US government user data to China, warnings of malware/vulnerabilities in the models.
- **China's response**: foreign ministry spokesperson Mao Ning (10-20) — protested "overstretching national security," vowed to defend Chinese firms' interests.

## Why it matters

1. **Supply-chain diligence widens**: "third-party products/services included" means even indirect use — a tool you use that embeds DeepSeek technology — falls in scope. A precedent forcing full dependency-tree diligence for builders selling into government/public projects.
2. **State intervention, two-sided**: 10-12 UK AISI blocked then restored frontier-model access; 10-19 UK CSRB put enforcement powers on agent behavior; 10-20 US DHS ordered a foreign model's removal — within eight days, "model access control" hardened into state power on both sides of the Atlantic. Model choice is becoming a geopolitical choice, not just a technical one.
3. **The open-weights paradox**: DeepSeek is the flagship open-weights lab. "Open means transparent" and "open means uncontrollable" are two sides of one coin — this order chose the latter. Builders betting on open weights must now ask "open from *where*."

## Limitations (explicit)

- Applies to federal civilian agencies — not directly to the private sector or state governments (though the procurement-market signal is separate).
- No removal deadline specified — actual removal pace is unknown.
- The concrete evidence behind DHS's "multiple threat vectors" is not public — the threat's substance rests on DHS's claims.
- The 10-20 Federal Register publication is the news hook — the order itself dates to 7-24 (a tracked follow-up from yesterday's dropped candidate).
