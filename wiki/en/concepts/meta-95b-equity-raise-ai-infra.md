# Meta's Up to $95B Equity Raise to Fund AI Infrastructure

## TL;DR

On 2026-10-26 Meta announced an equity raise of up to $95B to fund AI data-center and compute expansion: an immediate ~$35B offering (about $22.5B of Class A common stock plus about $12.5B of mandatory convertible preferred stock) and a separate at-the-market program of up to $60B starting in 2027, selling new non-voting Class C shares. Nvidia, AMD, and Broadcom indicated interest in buying up to $5B. Meta raised its 2026 capex guidance to $135–145B, and Q3 free cash flow turned negative (-$2.12B) for the first time in over a decade.

## Key facts

- **Structure**: the first tranche is ~$22.5B of Class A common stock sold through forward sale agreements with Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs (settlement on or before 2027-11-19) plus ~$12.5B of mandatory convertible preferred stock (250M depositary shares, Nasdaq ticker "METAM" planned, auto-converting around 2029-08-15). The second tranche is an approximately $60B ATM program selling the new non-voting Class C shares, not expected to start until Q1 2027 — no agreement signed yet, execution not assured.
- **Chip-supplier participation**: Nvidia, AMD, and Broadcom — all Meta chip suppliers — expressed interest in up to $5B of the Class A offering; the indications are non-binding.
- **Spending scale**: 2026 capex guidance $135–145B (lower end raised from $130B). Nine-month 2026 capex $83.1B vs $48.3B a year earlier (+72%). Total future spending commitments ~$825B, mostly data centers, AI infrastructure, and compute.
- **Cash-flow reversal**: Q3 free cash flow -$2.12B, the first negative quarter in over ten years (vs +$10.63B a year earlier). Nine-month 2026 buybacks: $0 vs $26.3B in 2025.
- **Earnings context**: Q3 revenue $64.6B (vs $63.37B expected), Q4 revenue guidance $73–76B. Shares rose 1.2% pre-market on the announcement but are down 2.7% year-to-date through 2026-10-25. At Friday's $642.62 close, selling the full $95B implies ~5.8% dilution (~147.8M shares).
- **Governance note**: Class C shares carry no votes, so the new stock does not touch the Zuckerberg entities' 56.1% voting power.

## Why it matters

1. **Capital, not models, is the bottleneck**: even a cash-generative giant now needs to issue stock to fund AI infrastructure — the constraint has moved from model quality to capital formation.
2. **Debt vs equity split**: CoreWeave-style debt vs Meta-style equity — the same AI boom is producing different financing structures, and the choice ties into governance (non-voting shares).
3. **Supplier-customer capital links**: chip suppliers buying into their customer's equity raise — hardware supply chains and capital are becoming intertwined.

## Limitations (explicit)

- The $60B ATM is a ceiling, not a committed raise: no agreement signed, execution not assured.
- The chipmakers' $5B interest is non-binding.
- The 5.8% dilution figure is a Tensorust estimate at the prior close; actual dilution depends on sale prices.
