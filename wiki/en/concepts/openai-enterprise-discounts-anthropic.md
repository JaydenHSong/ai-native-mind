# OpenAI's Discount Offensive Against Anthropic's Enterprise Customers

## TL;DR

The Information reported on 2026-10-24 that OpenAI is offering Anthropic's enterprise customers discounts of 20–40% off list prices — in some cases far more — led by its Codex coding tool, and the workloads are moving: Rippling's coding share flipped from Anthropic 57% / OpenAI 16% in May to OpenAI 52% / Anthropic 18% recently. Replit, DataCamp, and Atomicwork are also shifting. Anthropic refuses to extend discounts without larger total commitments, and faces its expected IPO next month with Q3 growth slowing to 45%.

## Key facts

- **Discount structure**: 20–40% off list prices for enterprise AI models including Codex; some companies received larger effective reductions (up to ~90% in effective cost terms). Free credits and seat doublings run alongside (a US bank with $7B+ in assets reportedly got ChatGPT seats doubled to 500 at no extra charge, under $7 per user).
- **Rippling reversal**: coding-work share, May → recent: OpenAI 16% → 52%, Anthropic 57% → 18%. CEO Parker Conrad said cost drove the switch.
- **Replit move**: moved most of its AI spending to OpenAI after a discount of more than 30% on a contract worth over $100M a year, after Anthropic declined to extend its prior discount without a bigger commitment. CEO Amjad Masad said onstage on October 16 that Replit was no longer working with Anthropic, then softened it to "model agnostic" with "OpenAI now our preferred partner." Replit's documentation still lists seven Claude models, so a complete split is unconfirmed.
- **DataCamp and Atomicwork**: DataCamp was offered 40% off conditional on adopting Codex, with an estimated 80–90% cost reduction if the deal extended; Atomicwork is training engineers on Codex via free credits from OpenAI and Khosla Ventures.
- **Financial contrast**: Anthropic Q2 revenue $11.5B against $5.6B in compute costs; Q3 growth 45%, slowed from Q2. OpenAI projects compute spending above revenue this year — the discounts buy share at negative margins, per the reporting.
- **Counter-evidence**: Anthropic retook the lead on OpenRouter by spend after Claude Opus 5.5, and is roughly level with OpenAI among Ramp customers. Neither company confirmed the figures.

## Why it matters

1. **Price, not capability, decides enterprise deals**: in this phase, discount terms — not benchmark gaps — are moving enterprise customers.
2. **Switching is measurable**: Rippling's 16%→52% is a real-world rate and magnitude for model migration — multi-model design is a survival condition, not a nicety.
3. **Loss-leading share buys**: OpenAI is discounting while projecting compute spend above revenue — today's prices do not guarantee tomorrow's.

## Limitations (explicit)

- Based on The Information's reporting and customer-CEO statements; neither OpenAI nor Anthropic confirmed the figures.
- Replit's "split" is disputed: CEO statements and documentation disagree, so a complete break is unconfirmed.
- The 80–90% DataCamp figure is the CEO's estimate for a conditional, extended deal, not a list-price cut.
