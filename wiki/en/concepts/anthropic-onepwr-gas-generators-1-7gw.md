# Anthropic Takes Over OnePWR's 1.7 GW Gas Generator Orders for About $3.4B — Largest Known Power-Equipment Buy by an AI Lab

## Start here

**Analogy**: imagine a restaurant that needs gas stoves, but ordering one means waiting two years. So instead it buys someone else's order, placed two years ago. Expensive — but the wait itself has become the bottleneck for the business.

| Term | Meaning |
|------|------|
| **OnePWR Solutions** | A San Antonio, Texas-based supplier of power equipment and microgrids |
| **1.7 GW** | 1.7 gigawatts — roughly enough power for 1.3 million homes |
| **Power-equipment purchase** | Buying generators outright and installing them to power a data center, instead of buying electricity from the grid |

## One-line definition

On 2026-11-08, the Financial Times reported that Anthropic agreed in late September to take over 1.7 gigawatts of heavy-duty gas-generator orders that OnePWR Solutions had placed with Caterpillar, for about $3.4 billion. The FT called it the largest known single purchase of power-generation equipment by an AI lab. Anthropic and OnePWR declined to comment, and Reuters said it could not independently verify the report.

## Key facts

- **Deal structure**: Anthropic took over orders OnePWR had placed with Caterpillar about two years earlier but needed financing to complete. Anthropic did not acquire OnePWR itself, and none of its employees transferred, but the deal includes the engineering and construction work needed to install the generators. Investment bank Piper Sandler lists a $3.4 billion sale of OnePWR's power-generation platform in September — to an unnamed AI lab.
- **Scale and unit cost**: 1.7 GW for about $3.4 billion — roughly $2 million per megawatt. OnePWR said in February that it held more than 400 MW of equipment in inventory and expected more than 800 MW over the following 24 months, about 1.2 GW in total — roughly 500 MW less than the reported 1.7 GW. No explanation for the difference has been given.
- **Background — power access is the constraint**: Anthropic's S-1 (October 26) says some planned data centers rely on dedicated on-site natural-gas generation and that Anthropic bears the fuel cost. The S-1/A (November 2) warns that access to electricity limits data-center expansion in several markets, and that generators, turbines, substations and transmission parts have faced extended delivery times, price increases, tariffs and rationing. On February 11, Anthropic committed to pay 100% of the grid upgrades needed to connect its data centers and to bring net-new power generation online.
- **Unconfirmed**: no sites or delivery schedule have been disclosed. It is not known whether the $3.4 billion sits inside the $895.4 billion of commitments disclosed in the S-1/A.

## Why it matters (solo-developer view)

1. **The power race has reached "buying someone's order"**: after land and lease backstops, labs are now buying generator order books outright. The bottleneck in AI infrastructure is moving from chips to the grid and to generation.
2. **The IPO filing's warning is becoming real**: the S-1 says equipment "must be ordered years in advance" — and Anthropic is now solving that risk with cash. For investors, deals like this are the substance filling in the $895.4 billion commitment figure.
3. **The environmental cost is the other axis**: the IEA estimated in its October 2026 report that annual emissions from data-center electricity could rise from about 150 million tonnes of CO2 to 350 million tonnes by 2035. Buying gas generators directly sits in the middle of that argument.

## Limitations (stated)

- One originating report (the FT); Reuters said it could not verify it independently. Anthropic, OnePWR and Caterpillar have not commented.
- The 1.7 GW figure is larger than OnePWR's publicly stated February inventory (about 1.2 GW) — the mismatch is unresolved.
- With no sites, delivery schedule or confirmation of whether the $3.4 billion is inside the $895.4 billion, the deal's weight inside Anthropic's power plan cannot yet be assessed.
