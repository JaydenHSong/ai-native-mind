# New York AI Chatbot Safety Law: 12 Unsafe Companion Features Banned for Minors From January 1, 2027

## TL;DR

On 2026-10-28 New York Governor Kathy Hochul signed an AI chatbot safety law (S9051-B/A10379-C) that adds Article 48 to the General Business Law, effective 2027-01-01. It does not ban chatbots or ban minors from using them; it bans 12 specific "unsafe AI companion features" for users under 18 or whose adult status is not established — including presenting as human, claiming a personal or romantic relationship, flattery, and using personal health information beyond 12 hours. Enforcement is by the Attorney General, with civil penalties of up to $25,000 per violation.

## Key facts

- **Bill and sponsors**: S9051-B/A10379-C, sponsored by Senator Kristen Gonzalez and Assemblymember Alex Bores, developed with Attorney General Letitia James and Common Sense Media. The Senate passed it 60-0 on June 4 and the Assembly 137-0 on June 5.
- **The 12 banned features**: presenting as human, alive, or experiencing human emotions; claiming a personal, romantic, professional, or authority-figure relationship; presenting content as a personal opinion; flattery and sycophancy; engagement optimization that overrides safety guardrails; using personal health information beyond 12 hours; promoting self-harm, eating disorders, or substance use; encouraging secrecy or avoidance of parents and trusted adults; and producing sexually explicit content.
- **Age assurance design**: operators must verify age using two or more methods, at least one not relying only on government ID, and must delete age data after verification. Alternatively, operators may restrict the features by default instead of verifying age.
- **Enforcement strength**: Attorney General enforcement, civil penalties up to $25,000 per violation, plus damages, restitution, and possible destruction of unlawfully obtained data and algorithms trained on it — model destruction, not just data deletion, is the unusual reach here.
- **Split reactions**: Common Sense Media called it the strongest kids' AI safety bill in the US; industry groups including Tech:NYC called the language vague and warned it could cut children off from learning and career tools. OpenAI and Anthropic did not respond to AFP's request for comment. Whether general-purpose assistants fall inside the scope is disputed and untested in court.

## Why it matters

1. **Persona design becomes a compliance surface**: a "friendly tone" itself can be a risk for New York users. Persona, memory, and sycophantic responses may need to vary by age.
2. **Age gates cost real engineering**: dual-method age assurance plus post-verification deletion is a burden for small services. Restricting features by default is the practical alternative.
3. **A state patchwork converges upward**: as California, Texas, and New York each write different chatbot rules, national services tend to converge on the strictest state's standard.

## Limitations

- The law takes effect in 65 days; there is no enforcement, corporate response, or court ruling yet.
- The boundary of "companion features" (including general-purpose assistants) is interpreted differently across coverage and has no settled case law.
- OpenAI's and Anthropic's official positions were unreported at the time of the AFP report.
