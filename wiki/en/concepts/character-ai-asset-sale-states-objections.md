# Character.AI Asset Sale — Bidding Procedures Approved Over States' Objections

## TL;DR

On October 30, 2026, Delaware bankruptcy judge Brendan Shannon approved Character.AI's bidding procedures over objections from state attorneys general. The company returns to court November 30 to seek approval of asset sales. Disney's $20 million stalking-horse bid covers a non-exclusive licence to chatbot technology — not the platform or user data, including chat history.

## Key facts

- **Ruling**: bidding procedures approved despite state objections; the sale-approval hearing is set for November 30, 2026.
- **Disney's bid**: $20 million for a non-exclusive licence to Character.AI's chatbot technology. The platform itself and user data, including chat history, are not part of the sale.
- **States' argument**: Disney stacked the deck by hiring most of Character.AI's staff before the bankruptcy, including its ex-CEO, and negotiating legal releases. Shannon did not approve those releases at this hearing, saying they can be challenged later.
- **Scale of the hire**: Disney hired 66 of Character.AI's 86 employees and expects to pay them $180 million over three years. Character.AI was losing $8–9 million per month.
- **Children's records**: a privacy ombudsman for children's chat records is likely to be appointed.

## Why it matters

1. **Bankruptcy does not end safety liability**: the technology sale proceeds under the shadow of child-safety litigation, but releases are a separate question to be litigated later. In AI wind-downs, the asset deal and the liability cleanup run on separate tracks.
2. **The acqui-hire, replayed**: 66 hires plus $180 million over three years is effectively the substance of the transaction. Talent acquisition inside bankruptcy proceedings is now a recurring pattern.
3. **Data left out**: excluding chat history from the sale sets a line — user conversations are not automatically a saleable asset. That matters for any chatbot product's shutdown design.

## Limitations

- The count of objecting states is inconsistent within the Reuters report itself (41 states in the text versus a coalition attorney speaking for 40 states); use "40–41 states."
- This page covers only the procedures approval. The November 30 sale-approval outcome is still pending.
