# Menlo Ventures 2026 State of Enterprise AI — $176.8B in Spending, Agents at 80% of Tokens

## Start here

**Analogy**: the number of companies using AI grew only a little, but what those companies spend grew more than five-fold. And the biggest 'customer' of AI is no longer a person — it is an agent. The restaurant's diners have been replaced by delivery robots.

| Term | Meaning |
|------|------|
| **Deployment spending** | Money enterprises spend actually using AI, as opposed to building models |
| **OpenRouter** | A routing platform that connects developers to many models through one gateway |

## One-line definition

Published 2026-11-03: Menlo Ventures' fourth State of Enterprise AI report put enterprise AI deployment spending at $176.8B as of September 2026 (5.4x the $33B of a year earlier) and agents at 80% of OpenRouter tokens.

## Key facts

- **Spending**: enterprise AI deployment spending of $176.8B (September 2026), 5.4x the $33B of 2025, accelerating from 3.9x growth the year before.
- **Agent consumption**: on OpenRouter, agents generate 80% of all tokens (130.9 trillion per week, about 7x human usage). After overtaking human traffic in February 2026, agent traffic grew 37x, versus 5x for human usage. The crossover followed the launches of Opus 4.6 and GPT-5.3-Codex.
- **App market**: enterprise AI app spending of about $77.3B (from $19B). Coding ($34.0B) and chatbots ($25.0B) make up 76%.
- **Concentration**: Anthropic, OpenAI and Google together take 69% of deployment spending (from 50%). Within enterprise LLM API spending, Anthropic rose from 40% to 55%, OpenAI from 27% to 31%, and Google fell from 21% to 9%.
- **Growth driver**: the share of companies using AI rose only from 55% to 65%. The surge came from existing users spending much more, not from new adopters.
- **Method and limits**: a Morning Consult survey of about 400 US enterprise decision-makers (September 8–26, 2026, overseeing $4B+ in AI spending), plus a bottom-up market model. Dollar totals are modeled estimates. Menlo discloses that it invests in Anthropic and OpenRouter. The figures measure spending, not productivity or profit outcomes.

## Why it matters (solo-developer view)

1. **The customer is now a machine**: with agents producing 80% of tokens, API pricing, rate limits and machine-readability are the sales channel.
2. **Demand is mapped**: coding and chatbots are three-quarters of app spending. For enterprise-facing solo work, workflow gaps in those two areas are the most validated demand.
3. **Concentration demands abstraction**: three vendors at 69% means model choice quickly becomes vendor lock-in. A routing or abstraction layer is negotiating leverage.

## Limitations (stated)

- Dollar totals are Menlo's modeled estimates, not audited accounts.
- OpenRouter is one channel, not the whole market, and Menlo has an investment relationship with it.
- Whether the spending paid off in productivity or profit cannot be judged from this report.
