# Jensen Huang Reinstated — NVIDIA Board Reverses Course 11 Days After His Resignation

## Start here

**Analogy**: the lord of a castle he built over 33 years stepped down — and 11 days later, he is lord again. Just a week earlier the board had said "never again," then changed its mind over a weekend. The president spoke up for his return, and a petition with more than 180,000 signatures backed it. The day he returned, the stock jumped more than 30%. But the investigations around him (subpoena, accounting probe, export-control questions) are still open — the seat is back, the problems are not over.

| Term | Meaning |
|------|------|
| **Reinstatement** | Returning a departed person to their former position |
| **Unanimous vote** | A vote in which every board member agrees |
| **Interim CEO** | A temporary chief executive until a permanent successor is named |

## One-line definition

On November 23, 2026, NVIDIA's board voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as president, CEO, and director, effective immediately — an abrupt reversal 11 days after he resigned over revelations of two extramarital affairs, one involving an alleged Chinese spy. The board said it had "received input from senior levels of the United States government" and is confident his return poses no national security risk.

## Key points

- **The decision**: a unanimous board vote, effective immediately. Chair Harvey C. Jones said, "The board believes Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers."
- **The scale of the reversal**: just a week earlier, on the earnings call, Jones said the board had "unanimously accepted" Huang's resignation and "does not intend to reinstate" him. Interim CEO Colette Kress said on November 18 that reinstatement was off the table. A series of discussions and meetings with Huang over the weekend flipped the decision.
- **Trump's involvement**: President Trump wrote on Truth Social that "The NVIDIA Board of Directors has made the correct and proper decision to return Jensen Huang to the position of CEO," and told CNBC he had persuaded the board. A petition calling for reinstatement drew more than 180,000 signatures, including Elon Musk's.
- **Market reaction**: NVDA jumped about 33% in early trading on the announcement (Reuters figure), with the Nasdaq up 6% and the S&P 500 up 4%. It was the rebound immediately after the Nasdaq's 5.28% plunge on November 20.
- **Risks remain**: the investigations continue despite the reinstatement. Huang is scheduled to testify before three House committees on December 1. The California subpoena, the reported SEC accounting probe, and export-control violation allegations are all still pending. The US Department of Justice has said the spy allegation was not substantiated (China also denies it), and the US government conveyed to the board that his return poses no national security issue.

## Why it matters (solo-developer lens)

1. **Leadership-gap risk, resolved and reignited**: one axis of the November 20 Nasdaq plunge — the NVIDIA leadership vacuum — was resolved in 11 days, and the AI rally reignited. But the structure is unchanged from [[concepts/nvidia-huang-ousted-ceo]]: the "silicon heart" of AI infrastructure is still tied to one person's governance.
2. **A question about board governance**: a board that said "no intention to reinstate" reversed itself unanimously within a week. Outside pressure (the president, a petition, the share price) appears to have moved its judgment. The question of board independence remains separate from the reinstatement itself.
3. **The next checkpoint moves**: December 1 House testimony and the pending subpoena and SEC probe — reinstatement is not the end of the investigations. For NVDA watchers, the question shifts from "who is CEO" to "how far do the investigations go."

## Limits (stated explicitly)

- The NVDA gain differs by outlet — about 33% in early trading (Reuters), 34% (New York Post), up to 36.6% intraday (SCMP video). Do not read it as a single fixed number.
- "Alleged spy" reflects US officials' assessment and press reporting; the DOJ has said it was not substantiated, and China denies it.
- The extent of Trump's involvement (persuasion vs. conveying views) rests on the president's statements and reporting; the board's internal deliberations have not been made public.
- confidence is medium — multiple first-party reports (Reuters, WSJ, CNN) cross-check, but the board's internal discussions and the government's input remain undisclosed.
