# Samsung Q3 Record: 107.44 Trillion Won Operating Profit, Semiconductors at 99% of It

## TL;DR

Samsung Electronics confirmed record third-quarter results on 2026-10-29: revenue of 195.25 trillion won (+126.9% year over year) and operating profit of 107.44 trillion won (+783.1%), the fourth straight quarter of records and the first time a Korean company has passed 100 trillion won in quarterly operating profit. The semiconductor division earned 106.5 trillion won — about 99% of group profit — while the device division lost money for a second straight quarter. Shares still fell about 4–5% in Seoul, because the results matched known guidance and new buyback details were deferred to a January board meeting.

## Key facts

- **Group results**: revenue 195.2532 trillion won (+13.85% quarter over quarter), operating profit 107.4405 trillion won (+20.06% quarter over quarter), operating margin 55.0%. Net profit was about 75.1 trillion won, below some consensus figures (about 87.4 trillion won from FnGuide in one account).
- **Semiconductor dominance**: Device Solutions revenue 147.1 trillion won, operating profit 106.5 trillion won (margin about 72.4%). That margin is below SK hynix's 79.7% because the division also includes foundry and System LSI.
- **Memory supercycle**: memory revenue 140.7 trillion won (+427% year over year) on HBM and server DRAM demand. HBM4 revenue more than tripled quarter over quarter to about 60% of HBM revenue. Samsung said 2027 HBM supply volume is already sold out at agreed target prices.
- **The device paradox**: the DX division (smartphones, TVs, appliances) reported revenue of 52.1 trillion won but its second straight quarterly loss, as soaring memory prices raised its own input costs.
- **Returns and investment**: a third-quarter dividend of 30 trillion won was confirmed; of the 90–110 trillion won annual shareholder-return plan, 35 trillion won is already paid or decided, with the rest set at the end-January 2027 board meeting. Full-year capital spending is 84.4 trillion won, mostly semiconductors.

## Why it matters

1. **Memory prices are AI costs**: HBM sellouts and price surges flow into cloud GPU prices and inference costs. The longer the supercycle lasts, the heavier the cost burden on small AI services.
2. **"Record earnings, falling shares" as a lesson**: the market reacted not to the size of the good news but to what was new in it (buyback details). Novelty, not magnitude, moved the price.
3. **Supply concentration**: two Korean memory makers now effectively set a large part of the world's AI cost base. Diversification and inventory strategy are part of cost management.

## Limitations

- Whether net profit "missed" depends on which consensus is used; operating profit itself matched guidance.
- US-dollar conversions vary from about $75B to $80B depending on the exchange-rate basis, so this page uses won figures.
- The 2027 HBM sellout carries the qualifier "at agreed target prices" in the company's statement.
