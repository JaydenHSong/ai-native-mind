# SpaceX $74B AI Supercomputing Campuses — Texas and Arizona, Power and Land Are the Bottleneck (Nov 12)

## Start here

**Analogy**: building a fast car needs more than an engine. You need the factory that builds the car, the power plant that runs the factory, and the neighbourhood where the workers live. SpaceX's $74B is a plan to build the 'factory + power plant + neighbourhood', not the 'engine'. The AI competition's bottleneck is moving from chips to land, power and people.

| Term | Meaning |
|------|------|
| **Supercomputing campus** | A large-scale AI compute facility complex — data centres, power and cooling gathered on one site |
| **Self-generation** | A facility generating its own electricity instead of drawing from the public grid |
| **County approval** | Development permission from a county, the US local-government unit — the Arizona site has not received it yet |
| **Permanent jobs** | Regular operating jobs that remain after construction ends |

## One-line definition

On 2026-11-12, SpaceX said it plans to invest more than $74 billion in two supercomputing campuses: more than $24 billion at Starbase, Texas, and about $50 billion in Cochise County, Arizona. A rocket company widening its position into AI infrastructure construction — an investment 'plan', not money already spent.

## Key facts

- **Scale**: more than $74 billion in total. Starbase (Texas) more than $24 billion, Cochise County (Arizona) about $50 billion.
- **Arizona condition**: county approval is still needed. About 5,500 construction jobs and about 1,000 permanent jobs are projected.
- **Power**: the Texas facility is designed to generate its own power rather than drawing from the public grid.
- **Plan vs execution**: this is the announcement of an investment 'plan', not completed or executed funding — several digests made that distinction explicit. Earlier the same week SpaceX had already said it was planning a gas-fired power plant and a supercompute facility at Starbase.
- **Context**: the AI infrastructure race is spreading beyond chips and cloud into a fight over power and land. It arrived the same week as Anthropic's IPO ($70–78 price range, pricing decision Nov 18) — another mega-scale infrastructure announcement.

## Why it matters (solo-developer view)

1. **The infrastructure race has moved to power and land**: how much power you can secure is becoming the bottleneck ahead of model performance. The same flow as Anthropic's OnePWR 1.7 GW generator acquisition (Nov 9) — labs now buy generators directly.
2. **SpaceX's identity is changing**: from a rocket company to an AI infrastructure builder. Binding xAI's Grok to SpaceX Super Intelligence reveals a vertical stack from launch to satellites to compute.
3. **An eye for plan vs execution**: $74 billion is the announced plan size. Actual execution depends on approvals, financing and construction. Look first at 'how much is confirmed', not the size of the number.

## Limitations (stated)

- It is at the plan stage. The Arizona site has not yet received county approval.
- Financing method and construction/completion schedules have not been disclosed.
- Beyond the original Reuters report, independently confirmed detail figures are limited.
