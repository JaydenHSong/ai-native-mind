# OpenAI Secures About $11B in a First Close — $1.25T Valuation, Down From the Original $30B Target at $1.4T

## Start here

**Analogy**: a company that set out to borrow $30 billion cut its plan to $20 billion, then took $11 billion as a first installment. The borrower says the round is not finished, and the lenders are not named. The company's value is up 47% since March, but below what it first asked for.

| Term | Meaning |
|------|------|
| **First close** | A stage in a funding round where part of the money is taken before the whole round closes |
| **Pre-money** | The value of a company before new capital is counted — counting it gives the post-money value |
| **Valuation** | The assessed value of the whole company |

## One-line definition

Bloomberg reported on 2026-11-06 that OpenAI secured about $11 billion in the first close of a new funding round, with the money wired on Friday, at a $1.25 trillion valuation. Reuters, CNBC and TechCrunch confirmed the same figures. The round is still open and could reach about $20 billion in the coming weeks. OpenAI has not confirmed it publicly.

## Key facts

- **First close**: according to Bloomberg's sources, about $11 billion was wired to OpenAI on Friday, November 6, at a $1.25 trillion valuation. Reuters reported the same figures from its own source, and CNBC independently confirmed the $11 billion portion. OpenAI declined to comment, and there has been no company announcement. The investors in this tranche were not confirmed in the Bloomberg report.
- **A shrinking target**: the original goal, as reported in September, was $30 billion or more at about a $1.4 trillion valuation; the target was cut to $20 billion on October 31, and the first close came in at $11 billion. If about $9 billion more arrives in the coming weeks, the total reaches $20 billion. The $1.25 trillion valuation is about 47% above the $852 billion set in the March round ($122 billion raised).
- **Participating investors**: Reuters, citing investors familiar with the deal, reported that SoftBank and MGX (the Abu Dhabi state-backed investment vehicle) took part, and the Financial Times reported that a UAE consortium including MGX is expected to be the largest participant in the overall round. Founders Fund, Coatue, Sequoia Capital and Andreessen Horowitz were also named as expected participants. None of this is confirmed.
- **Uncertainty in the figures**: CNBC reported on October 22 that the $1.25 trillion under discussion was a pre-money value, while Bloomberg and Reuters did not specify whether the $1.25 trillion in this report is pre- or post-money. The Information reported that OpenAI will brief investors on its third-quarter results next week.
- **Context**: Bloomberg tied the scaled-back raise to a selloff in public-market AI names. In the same week Alphabet recorded its largest-ever one-day drop on an earnings miss, and the Nasdaq was projected to lose more than $2 trillion of market capitalization in October. OpenAI's CFO told CNBC the company will keep investing in AI data centers regardless of the election results.

## Why it matters (solo-developer view)

1. **The funding line is continuing, but smaller**: a target that shrank from $30B to $20B to an $11B first close is a signal that funding for AI labs is no longer unconditional. With model development costs running into the hundreds of billions, the pace of fundraising is the ceiling on the pace of development.
2. **Valuation up, target down**: the valuation rose 47% from March while the raise shrank — investors accept the value but are committing less. That contradiction — value up, money in down — shows how the temperature of AI funding is changing.
3. **A funding gap is a compute gap**: Anthropic disclosed in the same week, via its S-1/A, third-quarter revenue of $16.96 billion and compute commitments of $895.4 billion. Both labs share the same structure — revenue growing, compute commitments growing faster. Funding news is the scoreboard of the compute race.

## Limitations (stated)

- All figures come from Bloomberg's sources; OpenAI has not confirmed them publicly. The investor list is unconfirmed.
- Bloomberg and Reuters did not specify whether $1.25 trillion is pre- or post-money, so direct comparison with March's $852 billion (post-money) needs care.
- The $20 billion total is a forecast for the coming weeks, not money already received.
