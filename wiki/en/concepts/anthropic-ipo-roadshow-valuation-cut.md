# Anthropic's IPO Roadshow Expected Next Week as Valuation Hopes Fall from $2T to $1–1.75T — Pre-Thanksgiving Listing Still the Goal

## Start here

**Analogy**: it is like asking $200 million for a house, then, as the market cools, settling for "somewhere between $100 million and $175 million." The house has not changed; the buyers' mood has. The seller still wants the deal done before Thanksgiving. One caution: this price was not set by the seller — it is what people say the buyers are whispering.

| Term | Meaning |
|------|------|
| **IPO roadshow** | The schedule of meetings with institutional investors before a listing, used to explain the company and collect orders |
| **S-1/A** | An amended registration statement filed with the US SEC — a blank price range means the price is still undecided |
| **Valuation** | The assessed value of the whole company, which changes with the price investors are willing to pay |

## One-line definition

On 2026-11-06–07, reporting from the Financial Times, CNBC, Bloomberg, Reuters and The Information said Anthropic is pushing ahead with a pre-Thanksgiving listing and is expected to start its IPO roadshow in the week of November 9, despite a sharp sell-off in tech stocks. The company aims to raise $80–100 billion at a $1.25–1.75 trillion valuation (targeting about $1.5 trillion), though some investors see $1–1.25 trillion as more realistic — well below the $2 trillion target of early October.

## Key facts

- **Roadshow and listing schedule**: The Information reported on November 6 that Anthropic will run the roadshow next week and still targets a listing in the week of November 16. JPMorgan is reported to be planning an investor presentation on November 13. Reuters, however, reported that investors question whether a roadshow starting next week leaves enough time for a November listing.
- **Valuation range**: according to investors cited by the Financial Times, the raise is $80–100 billion at $1.25–1.75 trillion, targeting about $1.5 trillion. CNBC reported on November 6 that one investor called $1–1.25 trillion a more likely target; Bloomberg reported prospective buyers discussing $1.2–1.3 trillion, some as low as $1.1 trillion. The Wall Street Journal reported that discussions fell as low as $1 trillion after Thursday's sell-off.
- **The slide**: a $2 trillion target in early October, about $1.5 trillion in mid-October (Axios), then $1–1.75 trillion on November 6. The private valuation from the May Series H was $965 billion ($65 billion raised), so even the lowered range sits above the last private mark. Gil Luria of D.A. Davidson told CNBC investors would expect pricing at least at the low end of earlier expectations.
- **Fundamentals**: under the S-1/A, revenue for the quarter ended September 30, 2026 was $17.0 billion, up 1,127% year over year. As of September 30, Anthropic had raised $141.1 billion in aggregate, with $95.6 billion in cash and equivalents and $36.3 billion in short-term investments. Ticker ANT, applied to list on the Nasdaq Global Select Market.
- **Market context**: CNBC reported that backlash against data centers after the midterms is a headwind for the IPO, and Kalshi traders priced the chance of an official IPO announcement before December 1 at 85% (about $4.99 million in volume).

## Why it matters (solo-developer view)

1. **The price tag on an AI lab now tracks market mood directly**: in six weeks the talk fell from $2 trillion to the low $1 trillions. It is not model performance setting the price but the question of whether AI spending earns money. Read next to OpenAI's $1.25 trillion round, it shows both frontier labs raising capital at the same price level.
2. **The listing itself has become the priority**: pushing for a pre-Thanksgiving debut even at a lower valuation signals urgency around post-listing funding, liquidity and talent retention. Whether the schedule holds is the next thing to watch.
3. **The reported numbers are rumors, not terms**: the S-1/A price range is still blank and Anthropic declined to comment. The $1.25–1.75 trillion and $1–1.25 trillion figures are "what investors say" from different sources. They should not be read as a set price.

## Limitations (stated)

- All figures come from anonymous sources and investor comments; Anthropic has issued no official statement on the timetable, valuation, offering size or share price, and declined to comment to Bloomberg, Reuters and CNBC.
- The reported ranges may rest on different share-count assumptions, so they are hard to compare directly. Reuters' doubts about the schedule remain unresolved.

## 2026-11-09 update — Marketing begins, investors push back, delay possible

Bloomberg's AI Today newsletter (November 9): Anthropic began marketing its IPO on November 9. But prospective buyers are discussing $1.2–1.3 trillion, some as low as $1.1 trillion. The Financial Times reported Anthropic's October revenue at about $81 billion annualized, up from $65 billion in July.

- **Schedule uncertainty**: Reuters reported that four people familiar with the talks said investors doubt a November listing is feasible, leaving open a delay if market pressure persists. Polymarket traders moved toward expecting the deal in December. Bloomberg had reported a debut before Thanksgiving on November 26; the Wall Street Journal reported the week of November 16.
- **Market context**: the Nasdaq 100 fell 6.4% on Thursday, wiping out $2.3 trillion in a single day (Bloomberg). OpenAI also raised $11 billion on November 6 at a $1.25 trillion valuation, below its original target.
- **How to read it**: the November 8 page's "expected to start next week" is now confirmed as "began on November 9," while the low end of the valuation discussion fell to $1.1 trillion and schedule uncertainty grew. The S-1/A price range is still blank.
- Reported the same day: [[concepts/anthropic-onepwr-gas-generators-1-7gw]] — Anthropic's $3.4B takeover of 1.7 GW in gas-generator orders.

## 2026-11-10 update — Amended S-1, $70–78 price range, $26B concurrent private placement

On November 10, Anthropic filed an amended S-1 that sets the offering price range for the first time: $70–78 per share, 825 million Class A shares. At the top of the range that implies a valuation of about $1.02 trillion (roughly 13.1 billion shares), and up to about $1.405 trillion fully diluted. The raise is $57.8–64.4 billion, with pricing expected on November 18 and trading on the Nasdaq as ANT.

- **Concurrent private placement**: at the same time, Anthropic will sell up to $26.0 billion of non-voting Class N shares — Google $10B, NVIDIA $10B, AMD about $2.5B, Broadcom/Micron/Microsoft about $1B each, TSMC about $500M. Net proceeds at the $74 midpoint are about $84.6 billion (raise plus private placement).
- **TSMC separately approved** up to $500M of Anthropic IPO shares and up to $500M of OpenAI preferred shares. TSMC's capex is about $40.93B; its dividend rose from NT$7.0 to NT$9.0.
- **How to read it**: the price range that was still blank on November 9 is now filled. The $70–78 range implies about $1.02 trillion, so the market's low-end discussion of $1.1–1.25 trillion has become the official ceiling. The $26B of strategic buyers — Google, NVIDIA, AMD, TSMC and others — is the largest anchor book in the IPO, but a high number of shares at a lower valuation is dilution. The November 8 page's "valuation $1–1.75T" now reads as a confirmed range with a midpoint near $74.
- Launched the same day: [[concepts/claude-fable-5-2-mythos-5-2]] — Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2, the flagship release paired with the filing.

## 2026-11-11 update — Amended prospectus withdrawn, $1.4T fully diluted valuation clarified

Anthropic's outside counsel withdrew the amended prospectus sent to banks on November 10, saying some pricing details were incorrect (Daily Post of AI, November 10, citing Reuters). Reuters Breakingviews (November 10) clarified the amended figures: about $1.4 trillion fully diluted at the $78 top of the range, about $1 trillion undiluted. The raise of up to $64.4 billion (825 million shares at $70–78), the separate $26B private placement, a November 19 listing and November 18 pricing are unchanged.

- **How to read it**: the numbers are the same as in the November 10 update — about $1.02 trillion undiluted to about $1.405 trillion fully diluted. What changed is that the dependence on the share-count assumption is now explicit at headline level: the valuation reads as $1T or $1.4T depending on which count is used. The withdrawal itself did not change the price range; the final range is set at pricing on November 18.

## 2026-11-12 update — Price range and schedule confirmed, Q3 revenue $17B disclosed, TSMC approves $1B

The price range of $70–78, 825 million Class A shares and Nasdaq ticker ANT are now confirmed. Pricing is set for November 18 (US Eastern) and trading is expected to begin November 19 (subject to regulatory approval). The offering raises up to about $64.4 billion, and up to about $90.4 billion including the separate $26B private placement. Reuters puts fully diluted market capitalisation at about $1.33 trillion at the midpoint and about $1.4 trillion at the top. The November 11 note about the withdrawn prospectus did not change the price range — the November 12 reports now treat that range as confirmed terms.

- **Fundamentals confirmed**: Q3 2026 revenue of $17 billion (+1,127% year over year, +47% quarter over quarter), $33.2 billion for the first nine months. Run-rate of about $81 billion as of October 31 (up from $71 billion at the end of September). A nine-month net loss of about $104 billion was reported (Motley Fool). CNBC-cited reporting also says 20% of the offering is allocated to retail investors.
- **TSMC's separate move**: TSMC's board approved up to $500M into Anthropic at the IPO price and up to $500M in OpenAI preferred shares (Nov 10), a combined ceiling of $1B. The Anthropic leg may overlap TSMC's share of the $26B private placement — the filings do not spell out whether it is the same commitment — and an approval is not a completed purchase.
- **How to read it**: the numbers are the same as in the November 10 update — about $1.02 trillion undiluted to about $1.405 trillion fully diluted. What changed is that the schedule (pricing Nov 18, trading Nov 19) and the fundamentals (Q3 revenue $17B, run-rate $81B) are now confirmed at filing and reporting level. It lands in the same week as the NVIDIA CEO ouster ([[concepts/nvidia-huang-ousted-ceo]]), concentrating AI-sector news. Sources: thecircuitry, Phillip Nova, Motley Fool, Morningstar/Dow Jones, Nov 11–12.
