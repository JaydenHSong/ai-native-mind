# Trump Threatens a 'Tremendous Tax' on US Firms Building AI Data Centers Abroad (Nov 20)

## Start here

**Analogy**: "Build our companies' factories inside our country. Build them outside and we will tax you enormously" — that is what the president said about data centers, the factories of the AI era. The targets are big companies such as Amazon, Google, and Microsoft. But how much the tax would be, and under what law, has not been decided at all. So far it is only words.

| Term | Meaning |
|------|------|
| **Data center** | A huge building holding tens of thousands of computers that run AI — the factory of the AI era |
| **Congressional legislation** | A new tax needs Congress to pass a law — the president's words alone are not enough |

## One-line definition

At a White House press conference on November 20, 2026, President Trump said he would stop US companies from building data centers abroad and impose a "tremendous tax" on them. The remark came right after his return from a visit to China, and it directly targets companies — Amazon, Google, Microsoft, Meta, Oracle — that have promised billions of dollars for data centers overseas.

## Key points

- **The remark**: "We're not going to allow our companies to build data centers outside of our country" — a tax threat against companies that build abroad.
- **Targets**: Amazon, Google, Microsoft, Meta, Oracle. These companies are building data centers overseas in search of cheaper land, labor, and power, and to avoid permitting fights in the US.
- **Limits**: no tax rate, legal basis, or timetable was given. Imposing a tax requires legislation by Congress.
- **Market reaction**: the remark came on a day the Nasdaq fell sharply, giving back its entire 2026 gain (see [[concepts/nasdaq-ai-rout-nvidia-sec-probe]]).
- **Place in the policy flow**: together with the November 16 BIS remote-compute rule and the Senate's November 19 permitting-relief bill, it is the latest turn in US policy around where AI infrastructure sits and how it is financed.

## Why it matters (solo-developer lens)

1. **AI compute infrastructure is now itself a policy target**: where data centers — the physical base of model and agent competition — get built is now a presidential talking point. "Where does the computing happen" is a policy variable, not just a technical choice.
2. **The gap between words and law is the thing to watch**: no rate, basis, or schedule was given. For the threat to become a real tax it must pass through Congress — follow-up legislative moves matter more than the remark itself.
3. **Permitting relief and the overseas-build crackdown arrived in the same week**: the Senate's November 19 permitting-relief bill aims to make building inside the US easier, while the November 20 remark aims to stop building abroad. Carrot and stick point the same way — toward building inside the US.

## Limits (stated explicitly)

- A single-source Reuters report, with no tax rate, legal basis, or timetable given. This is a remark, not a policy.
- A tax requires legislation by Congress, so whether and when it is actually imposed is undecided.
- confidence is low — a single source, with no follow-up legislation or specifics yet.
