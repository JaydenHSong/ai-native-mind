# Intuit Cuts Credit Karma Outlook — the First Hard Number for LLMs Eating App Revenue

## Start here

**Analogy**: Credit Karma is an app that compares credit cards and loans for you. People now ask an AI "pick the right card for me" instead of opening the app. Revenue through the app fell, so owner Intuit cut its outlook: "we thought Credit Karma would grow 11–13% this year; now we expect 3–7%." The day "AI will eat existing apps" stopped being speculation and showed up as a listed company's earnings number.

| Term | Meaning |
|------|------|
| **Guidance cut (outlook cut)** | A company lowering its own forecast of how much it will earn |
| **SaaS** | Software sold as a subscription service |
| **Brokerage-style app** | An app that connects people with products (cards, loans, travel) and earns a fee |
| **AI displacement** | AI taking over the work — and revenue — of an existing service |

## One-line definition

On November 19, 2026, Intuit cut Credit Karma's growth outlook from **11–13% to 3–7%** in its Q1 2027 earnings call. Consumers now use LLMs to research financial products, and that directly hit Credit Karma's revenue, the company said. Intuit's shares fell **−14.18%** that day, the largest drop in the Nasdaq-100.

## Key points

- **The outlook cut**: Credit Karma growth outlook cut from 11–13% to 3–7%. Intuit's explanation: consumers use LLMs to research financial products, directly reducing revenue routed through Credit Karma.
- **The stock**: Intuit fell 14.18% on November 19, the largest single-day drop in the Nasdaq-100.
- **Why it matters**: TFTC called it "the first hard reported number showing AI destroying existing application-layer revenue." Until now, worries about AI displacing SaaS were speculation; this is the first case where it was written directly into a listed company's earnings guidance.
- **Source separation**: the outlook cut itself is cited from Intuit's earnings call (primary). The "first hard number" framing is TFTC's interpretation.

## Why it matters (solo-developer lens)

1. **"AI eats SaaS" is now a number**: until now it was worry and speculation; now a listed company's guidance carries it directly. Where [[concepts/ai-debt-boom-cracking]] covered overheating infrastructure investment, this is the second axis — revenue displacement in the application layer.
2. **Brokerage-style apps are hit first**: "gathering information and comparing it" — what financial-product search does — is exactly the job LLMs replace best. The same current runs through [[concepts/booking-vayo-travel-agent]] (travel brokerage) and [[concepts/agentic-commerce-gemini-tasks-klarna]] (agent commerce): fee-for-matching models shake first.
3. **The same day had numbers pointing the other way too**: Anthropic listed after raising a record $81.3B ([[concepts/anthropic-ipo-first-day-ant-97]]), while existing SaaS cut its outlook. Money flowing into AI labs and revenue draining from existing apps were printed on the market on the same day.

## Limits (stated explicitly)

- The outlook cut is cited from Intuit's earnings call, but the "because of AI" causality is Intuit's explanation — other factors (the economy, competition) may be mixed in.
- The "first hard number" framing is TFTC's interpretation — verifying the "first case" claim needs more cases to accumulate.
- confidence is medium — a primary earnings-call citation, but the causal attribution and "first case" framing are interpretive.
