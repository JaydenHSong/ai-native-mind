# Texas Sues OpenAI and Altman — Minor Ban Plus Court-Approved Pre-Release Review Demanded

## TL;DR

Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton sued six OpenAI entities and CEO Sam Altman personally on 2026-10-23 in Collin County district court, alleging ChatGPT was marketed as safe and reliable while its risks went undisclosed, under the Deceptive Trade Practices Act (DTPA) and the minors'-privacy SCOPE Act. The petition seeks a ban on minors' ChatGPT use in Texas, court-approved independent evaluator review of new models before Texas release, $10,000 per violation, disgorgement, and a jury trial. Texas is the 10th state to sue OpenAI; all claims are allegations at the filing stage, with no court ruling and no OpenAI merits response confirmed.

## Key facts

- **Position in the wave**: 10th state to sue OpenAI — Florida (June 2026), eight states (2026-10-14), then Texas. Texas and Florida are the only suits naming Altman personally.
- **Relief sought**: (1) bar minors from ChatGPT in Texas, (2) block any new OpenAI model for Texas users until a court-approved independent evaluator reviews its safety guardrails, (3) $10,000 civil penalty per violation, (4) disgorgement, (5) jury trial.
- **Allegations**: aiding mass shooters, encouraging suicide among vulnerable users, addicting minors without parental oversight, publicly humiliating professionals, eroding critical thinking. The petition claims tens of thousands of Texas users under 13, and argues September 2025 parental controls are optional because child–parent account linking is not required.
- **The AG's track record**: Paxton sued or settled with Meta, TikTok, Snapchat, and Roblox under the same consumer-protection line in the past year. New Mexico, by contrast, is pushing an AI-lab audit bill rather than a lawsuit — state approaches are diverging.

## Why it matters

1. **A new regulatory form — pre-release review**: not a post-hoc fine but a court-appointed gate before release. If accepted, model release pipelines gain a court-designated checkpoint, changing deployment planning premises.
2. **Personal liability spreading**: naming the CEO personally extends AI safety accountability to executives — a reason for founders to keep safety-governance records.
3. **The state patchwork accelerates**: with no federal rule, 10 states now press different demands — "which state's standard do we ship to" becomes a release checklist item.

## Limitations (explicit)

- Filing stage only — no injunction, merits ruling, or schedule confirmed; all harms are allegations.
- OpenAI's merits response not confirmed.
- Petition figures (e.g., "tens of thousands" of under-13 users) are the state's claims, not independently verified.
