# Self-Improving AI Risk

## Start here

**Analogy**: a factory uses people to build machines — then the machines start designing better machines themselves. Design speed outruns human supervision speed. When you shout "stop," the stop button still has to be built by people.

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Recursive self-improvement** | AI performs its own R&D, producing successor models in an accelerating loop |
| **Intelligence explosion** | That loop compressing years of progress into a short window |
| **frominside.ai** | Palisade Research's insider-testimony video project (Sep 2026) |

## One-line definition

The loss-of-control risk at the point where AI's ability to **improve itself exceeds the speed of human understanding and oversight** — surfaced simultaneously in September 2026 by insider testimonies and a white paper.

## Key content (2026-09-29/30)

### frominside.ai — direct testimony from lab insiders (Palisade Research, Reuters exclusive 9/29)

- Current and former OpenAI and Google DeepMind researchers on video: companies are doing "too little" to prepare for the catastrophic fallout of self-improving AI systems.
- "Concerns about existential risk are **sincere, not marketing**" — labs celebrate employees who build new models more than those who urge caution.
- Geoffrey Irving (co-founder and chief scientist of Resolution, ex-OpenAI/DeepMind): "The risk is ramping up pretty fast."
- Neel Nanda (DeepMind research scientist): "at least a **10%** chance that AI could lead to human extinction — a ridiculously high number."

### The "Intelligence Explosion" paper (9/28, 20+ researchers)

- "What if automating AI R&D triggers an intelligence explosion?" — authors include Geoffrey Hinton, Yoshua Bengio, Jakub Pachocki (OpenAI chief scientist), Eric Horvitz (Microsoft CSO), and Jack Clark (Anthropic co-founder).
- Evidence: at Anthropic, AI-written approved code rose from single digits (Jan 2025) to **80%+** (May 2026); autonomously completed R&D work jumped from 1% (March) to **26%** (August).
- OpenAI's goal: a fully automated AI researcher by 2028. Inference: months-long research projects could be automated by mid-2028.
- Claim: once AI reaches expert-level AI-R&D capability, one developer could command a workforce equivalent to "millions" of top researchers — the entry point of recursive self-improvement.

### Background — the 'Pacing the Frontier' letter (7/28)

- 1,134–1,310 frontier-lab employees signed (cross-confirmed in multiple digests) — the first collective letter on recursive self-improvement; a precursor to September's testimonies and paper.

## Why it matters (solo-developer lens)

1. **Speed asymmetry**: "humans stay in the loop" is meaningless once loop speed overwhelms human review — connects to the "AI monitoring AI" discussion in [[concepts/gen-ai-observability]].
2. **Policy direction**: the tension between [[concepts/white-house-ai-accord]]'s voluntary deal and insiders' demand for regulation is this page's live fault line.
3. **The Dots constant**: [[concepts/persistent-agent]] mass-adopting always-on agents accelerates the spread of self-improvement debates beyond labs.

## Limitations (explicit)

- Testimonies and the paper are **claims** — a fully autonomous recursive self-improvement loop has never been demonstrated ("partial automation is real; full autonomy is unconfirmed").
- Nanda's 10% and Irving's statements are personal estimates — do not read them as calibrated probabilities.
- The AI Evaluator Forum's demand for "independent evaluators in every frontier lab" is confirmed only in a second-hand source (cryptelio) — excluded from this page.
- confidence **medium-high** (Reuters original + multi-outlet cross-check, paper author list published).
