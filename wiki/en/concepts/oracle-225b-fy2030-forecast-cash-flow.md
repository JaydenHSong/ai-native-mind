# Oracle's $225B FY2030 Revenue Forecast: A $20B Fundraising Cap as the Answer to AI Debt Worries

## TL;DR

At its 2026-10-28 Investor Day, Oracle forecast at least $225B in revenue for fiscal 2030 — about 35% compound annual growth, above the $217.7B FactSet consensus. The market-moving part was not the revenue target but the funding cap: CFO Hilary Maxson said only $20B more in fundraising is needed to finish this cycle, split evenly between debt and equity. Shares rose about 11% to $144.54, the largest single-day gain since May.

## Key facts

- **2030 targets**: revenue of at least $225B, adjusted operating margin above 40%, adjusted EPS above $21. Fiscal 2027 guidance reaffirmed: revenue of at least $90B, adjusted EPS $8.10.
- **Cash flow timeline**: free cash flow turns positive in the second half of fiscal 2028 and exceeds $30B in fiscal 2029. Capital spending peaks in fiscal 2027–2028 and slows in fiscal 2029.
- **What supports the cap**: vendor financing, customer-supplied hardware, and customer prepayments reduce upfront cash needs. From fiscal 2029 Oracle expects to self-fund growth and bring net debt below 3.5x annual core earnings.
- **Starting point**: notes payable and other borrowings of $125.34B and cash of about $36.37B as of August 31, with a BBB- credit rating — the lowest investment-grade rung. Oracle had already raised $40B in debt and equity in the current fiscal year, including a $20B stock sale in the first quarter, before declaring this "final" $20B.
- **Market reaction**: Oracle rose about 11–13% on the day, and neocloud peers CoreWeave, Nebius, Applied Digital, and Iren rose 2.1–5%.

## Why it matters

1. **Neocloud pricing follows funding**: the financing capacity of Oracle, CoreWeave, and peers feeds directly into GPU and inference pricing. A funding cap can also read as "prices will rise to meet the cash flow schedule."
2. **How to evaluate a cloud vendor**: beyond the price list, check debt maturities and the free-cash-flow conversion schedule. A vendor's funding stress can surface as service changes or price increases.
3. **The AI-bubble debate gains an evidence pattern**: companies are now answering "debt-funded AI" criticism with caps and conversion dates. Future earnings become the test of whether those promises hold.

## Limitations

- All figures are forward-looking forecasts, not guarantees; the 2030 target is four years out.
- The $20B cap excludes refinancing of existing debt, so it must not be confused with total borrowing.
- Reported share gains (11–13%) vary by outlet and reference time.
