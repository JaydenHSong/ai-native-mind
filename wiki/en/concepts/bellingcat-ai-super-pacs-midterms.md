# Bellingcat Tracks AI Super PACs — $125M vs $40M, and AI Barely Appears in the Ads

## Start here

**Analogy**: an election that will decide AI rules, fought with ads that leave AI out and talk about prices and immigration instead. The money's intent and the ad copy run on separate tracks.

| Term | Meaning |
|------|------|
| **Super PAC** | A political committee that can accept unlimited donations for independent spending; coordination with candidates is banned |
| **501(c)(4)** | A social-welfare nonprofit with no donor-disclosure obligation |

## One-line definition

Bellingcat investigation, 2026-11-02: ahead of the midterms, Leading the Future ($125M, pro-deregulation) and Public First Action ($40M from Anthropic, pro-safety) faced off — but of 300+ ads analyzed, only a handful mentioned AI.

## Key facts

- **The camps**: Leading the Future (LTF) opposes regulations that 'constrain' AI innovation, funded with $125M total from Greg Brockman and his wife and from a16z and its founders, per FEC filings. Public First Action (PFA) demands safety regulation and guardrails, funded with $40M from Anthropic.
- **Structure**: LTF mainly disburses to other super PACs rather than advertising directly: about $52M disbursed so far, $20M to Think Big (Democratic targets) and $20M to American Mission (Republican targets). PFA, as a 501(c)(4), need not disclose donors or spending, but filings for its super PACs show $15.4M to Jobs and Democracy and $7.9M to Defending Our Values.
- **What the ads actually say**: Bellingcat analyzed 300+ online video ad campaigns from four super PACs, supporting or opposing 40 candidates. Only ads tied to two candidates mentioned AI; none mentioned data centers. Themes were the economy, cost of living, immigration, law enforcement and Trump/MAGA — and the same network's messaging flipped depending on the target audience.
- **The exception**: New York's Alex Bores primary, where AI was front and center. Jobs and Democracy ran 152 ads backing Bores, some mentioning AI safeguards; Think Big ran 22 ads opposing him, attacking Anthropic funding and 'dark money' rather than his AI stance.
- **Opacity**: the network channelled more than $36.4M to four newly created LLCs using Nevada mailboxes and office-supply stores as addresses. The Campaign Legal Center complained to the FEC in May that the super PACs evaded reporting rules through shell companies; the FEC has not ruled.

## Why it matters (solo-developer view)

1. **AI regulation is now a campaign-finance fight**: labs funding opposite camps means the rules' direction follows a money contest, not just a technical debate.
2. **Ad copy hides the agenda**: voters see no AI in the ads, yet AI rules arrive after the election. 'The topic missing from the ad' can be the real agenda.
3. **Disclosure has gaps**: 501(c)(4) routing and LLC pass-throughs mean filings alone do not reveal the full picture until the FEC rules.

## Limitations (stated)

- Funding totals are FEC-filing based; 501(c)(4)-routed money cannot be fully traced.
- Bellingcat reports no evidence of coordination between the super PACs and candidates.
- The voter survey (about three-quarters less inclined to back a candidate who took AI super PAC money) is a third-party poll citation.
